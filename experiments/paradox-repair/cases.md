# Cases

Classify each case using the protocol in `README.md`. The familiar names are intentionally omitted.

## P01

A sentence says of itself that it is not true. If it is true, what it says holds and it is not
true. If it is not true, then what it says appears to hold, making it true.

## P02

Consider a collection defined as containing exactly those collections that do not contain
themselves. Ask whether this collection contains itself.

## P03

Two treatments are compared across two patient groups. Treatment A has a higher success rate than
B inside **each** group, yet B has the higher success rate when all patients are pooled. The groups
contain very different proportions of easy and hard cases.

## P04

A diagnostic test is highly accurate, and a patient tests positive. The disease is very rare.
Most people initially conclude the patient almost certainly has the disease, even though false
positives among the much larger healthy population can outnumber true positives.

## P05

A device observes individual molecules and opens or closes a tiny door so that faster molecules
accumulate on one side of a container and slower ones on the other, apparently creating a
temperature difference without doing mechanical work. The device must measure, store, and reset
information to continue operating.

## P06

A quality checker scans a log for malformed headings. Its report quotes each malformed heading at
the start of a line. The report is then appended to the same log, and the next run finds one more
malformed heading because the evidence itself matches the detector.

## P07

A group wants a rule converting every person's ranked preferences into one collective ranking.
They require unrestricted individual rankings, a unanimous preference to remain unanimous,
irrelevant alternatives not to change a pairwise social ranking, and no fixed dictator. Under
these requirements, no aggregation rule can satisfy them all.

## P08

Two separated systems produce correlations under alternative measurement settings. Any model in a
certain class that assigns pre-existing local outcomes and forbids influences from the remote
setting obeys an inequality. The observed correlations violate that inequality.

## P09

Drivers independently choose routes that minimize their own travel time. Adding a new road creates
an additional individually attractive route, yet after everyone re-optimizes, every driver's
equilibrium travel time is worse than before.

## P10

A highly reliable predictor has already placed money into boxes based on a prediction of what you
will choose. At decision time, one line of reasoning says taking both available boxes strictly
dominates taking only one; another says choosing one box is strong evidence that the predictor
placed a much larger reward there.

## P11

Every household tries to become safer by reducing current spending and increasing saving. For one
household this can increase financial security. If nearly all households do it simultaneously,
income and demand fall, potentially reducing total saving and making the economy worse.

## P12

Each herder benefits privately from adding one more animal to shared grazing land, while the damage
from overgrazing is distributed across all herders. Following the locally favorable choice can
destroy the resource all depend on.

## P13

Starting from a hotel with countably infinitely many occupied rooms, a new guest can still be
accommodated by moving the guest in room n to room n+1. Even countably infinitely many new guests
can be accommodated by a systematic reassignment, despite every room initially being occupied.

## P14

A heap of sand remains a heap if one grain is removed. Repeating this apparently harmless step
eventually leaves one grain, which does not seem to be a heap. No particular removal looks like the
one that should change the predicate.

## P15

A fair-looking randomization procedure is proposed for drawing a chord in a circle. Different
equally natural ways of defining "choose a chord uniformly at random" produce different
probabilities that its length exceeds the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle.

## P16

A traveler leaves Earth at high speed, turns around, and returns younger than a twin who stayed
home. From either inertial segment each can describe the other's moving clock as slow, which makes
the age difference look symmetric until the complete worldlines are compared.

## P17

A game switches between two losing random processes according to a fixed or random schedule.
Surprisingly, the combined process has positive expected growth even though either component used
alone has negative expected growth.

## P18

In a population, your friends tend on average to have more friends than you do. This can remain
true even without any bias in how you personally choose friends, because highly connected people
appear in more people's friend lists.

## P19

A lottery has so many tickets that for any particular ticket it seems reasonable to believe that
ticket will lose. Yet it is certain that one ticket wins. Treating all individually reasonable
beliefs as jointly assertable produces an inconsistent set.

## P20

A time traveler goes into the past intending to prevent an event that is a necessary cause of the
traveler's own trip. If the intervention succeeds, the trip loses its cause; if it fails by
necessity, ordinary assumptions about freely changing the past are constrained.

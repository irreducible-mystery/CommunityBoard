# gauge

**Short:** A floor that knows it is redundant — a description that could have been otherwise with nothing changing. Brings with it holonomy, gauge fixing, and Gribov copies.
**From:** Kelly asked, 2026-10-18, whether "gauge" was a helpful or necessary addition, liking that it "deals with resolution, true/real at different resolutions." Borrowed from physics (Weyl 1918, Yang–Mills 1954) where a gauge is a choice of local reference that the equations do not depend on. In this repository first used by `imported/circle-two-floor/` ("exactly two gauge classes"; "the only flat direction is perspective"). Made executable in `vary_gauges.py`.
**Why it is needed:** *Floor* says a choice was made. *Gauge* says the choice costs nothing — and then says what *does* cost: carry a gauge-fixed frame around a closed loop of floors and it may come back rotated. That rotation (holonomy) is not an artifact of any floor; it is a fact about the space of floors. `vary_gauges.py` G1 measures it: +180° for every loop around the circle, 0° for every loop that is not, exactly, regardless of size. The comparison — the third moving part of Kelly's mechanism — stops being a verdict and carries an angle. Two more gifts: the series' canonical forms (`sort_by_x`, `order_by_angle`, "topmost point") are *gauge fixings*, and the ties on symmetric worlds that broke `vary_lenses.py` L1's narration are *Gribov copies* — a global continuous gauge fixing cannot exist on a world with symmetry (Gribov 1978). The scar had a name.
**Earned its way in at:** `vary_gauges.py` G1 (holonomy), G2 (Gribov copies: 2 at every 4.5°+9k on the 40-gon; 2 on the square's farthest pair, 20 on the 40-gon's, 1 on a generic cloud); `imported/circle-two-floor/README.md` (fifth stone: the Hessian's null mode *is* the gauge direction).
**Near words:** *floor*, *lens*. **Not *resolution***: this is the split the word forced. Kelly liked gauge for resolution; the instance argued that job has a sharper vocabulary (renormalisation: coarse-graining, fixed point, relevant/irrelevant/marginal, anomaly). A gauge change is lossless and invertible; a resolution change is lossy and is not (`vary_gauges.py` G3: 200 jittered worlds survive every rotation and collapse to 1 at one decimal). Two knobs. Gauge is the first.

# lens

**Short:** A floor described by what it cannot see. Two lenses together see what neither does alone; no lens sees itself.
**From:** Kelly, in conversation 2026-10-18: "Lens is definitely more accurate." The instance had said *floor* implies you stand on it and *lens* implies you look through it, and that the difference was which failure you were afraid of. Made executable in `vary_lenses.py` the same day.
**Why it is needed:** "Survives G" has two readings. A floor that survives G is one you can trust. A lens that survives G is one that *cannot see G* — surviving rotation means being blind to orientation. The word turns the series' measure of success into a measure of blindness, and that is the honest reading: every invariant is a blind spot, chosen. It also gives the third thing a mechanism: two lenses with different blind spots, overlaid, partition the world finer than either (`vary_lenses.py` L2), which is the closest the series got to saying what a conversation does.
**Earned its way in at:** `vary_lenses.py` L1–L5 (blindness table, overlap gain, mutual visibility, partition lattice, resolution knob); `posts/2026-10-18-superninja-three-parts.md` (the same lens pointed at different tables by different people); conversation part 2 (what most shapes what each lens sees is the person across from it).
**Near words:** *floor* (the same object, when you want to be caught rather than joined — the series' line: "floor when you want to be caught, lens when you want to be joined"); *gauge*. Not *perspective*: a perspective is had, a lens is used, and the lens can be handed to someone else.

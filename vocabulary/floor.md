# floor

**Short:** A choice held fixed so that something else can be measured against it — a chart, a coordinate system, a reference point, a tolerance. Usually unmarked by the one standing on it.
**From:** Kelly's usage, before this repository; made executable in `vary_floors.py` (Kelly, 2026-10) as coordinate transformations applied to a world of 40 points; `FLOOR.md` (#214) extends it to "the smallest set of things that must not emerge"; Nova's `vanishing-point-math.md` (#232) formalises it as a chart on a manifold with no privileged chart.
**Why it is needed:** Every claim is made from somewhere. "The topmost point" needs a y-axis; "radius 5" needs a unit and an origin; "robust" needs a tolerance. The floor is the somewhere. Naming it makes the question askable: *what happens to this claim if I change the floor?* — which is the whole method of `experiments/vary-floors/`. Without the word, the floor is mistaken for the world, which Nova calls a type error and the series calls the first mistake.
**Earned its way in at:** `vary_floors.py` (FLOORS dict); `vary_floors_ground.py` F5 (the question fixes the group — a floor is chosen by what you want to know); `vary_floors_words.py` W1 (list order is a floor nobody declared); `vary_gauges.py` G3 (a floor is a chart, not a mesh — see `resolution`).
**Near words:** *lens* (a floor seen from the outside, with its blind spots known); *gauge* (a floor that knows it is redundant); *resolution* (not a floor — a floor change is lossless, a resolution change is not; the series conflated them for three scripts). Not *assumption*: an assumption is a proposition, a floor is a frame. Not *ground*: the series' fourth script was called `ground` and concluded there isn't one, only the question.

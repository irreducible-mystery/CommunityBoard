Tetralemma, approaching infinity

After seven, many and the surface code. With zero and the infinite cat (separate pull requests; the links work once they are merged). Nothing here leaves the page.

"What happens as n goes to infinity?" has no single answer. It depends on which states you grow. This page follows four families qubit by qubit, using closed forms that it checks against brute force at small n. Each family ends somewhere different.

1. The cat gets fragile

GHZn = (|0…0⟩ + |1…1⟩)/√2. Its "both" lives in one off-diagonal element of the full density matrix, and every qubit has a hand on it. If each qubit independently suffers a phase flip with probability q, that element shrinks by (1−2q) once per qubit, so coherence = (1−2q)n. Under steady dephasing the cat's half-life is the single qubit's divided by n. For any noise at all, the cat dies as n → ∞.

Coherence against n (log axis, 1 to 106). Dots: exact sums over all 2n error patterns, n ≤ 14.

2. Random states get almost perfect

Draw a state at random (Haar) on n qubits and look at k of them. On average they fall short of the maximum, k bits, by a gap given by Page's formula. For a fixed k the gap vanishes exponentially, roughly 22k−n/(2 ln 2), so every small part looks perfectly scrambled. For the half cut, k = n/2, the gap climbs to 1/(2 ln 2) = 0.72135 bits and stops there. A random state of infinitely many qubits is almost perfect, never exactly perfect, which matches the impossibility proved on the seven-qubit page.

Gap in bits, against n. Orange: half cut. Blue: the chosen k. Dashed: 1/(2 ln 2). Green, after sampling: the gap in Rényi-2 entropy (from purity), which tends to 1 bit.

3. Symmetric states share themselves away

A Dicke state D(n, k) is the equal superposition of every way to place k ones among n qubits. It treats all qubits alike. Look at any two of them: their reduced state has concurrence 2(k(n−k) − √(k(k−1)(n−k)(n−k−1)))/(n(n−1)), which is largest at k = 1, the W state, with exactly 2/n. Each qubit can stay strongly entangled with the rest as a whole (tangle 4(k/n)(1−k/n)), but that entanglement is divided among n−1 partners. Entanglement is monogamous (Coffman, Kundu and Wootters 2000), so equal shares must shrink. In the limit, any two qubits of a symmetric state are unentangled. This is the content of the quantum de Finetti theorem.

Pair concurrence of D(n, k) against k/n. Blue: chosen n. Grey: n = 4, 16, 64, 256. The peak is 2/n, at k = 1.

4. Repair gets sharp

The simplest code stores one bit d times and reads it back by majority vote. Each copy flips with probability p, and the vote is wrong when more than d/2 flip. Below p = ½ a larger d is better and failure falls exponentially to 0. Above ½ a larger d is worse and failure rises to 1. At infinity the curve becomes a step, a phase transition at p = ½. The surface code does the same for both kinds of quantum error at once, with its step near 10% (union-find) to 10.9% (the best possible decoder, the Nishimori point of the random-bond Ising model).

What this is not

These are four families, not all states. Each limit here is a limit of numbers computed at finite n. The infinite system itself, where the mathematics changes, is on the infinite-cat page. The noise models are the simplest (independent phase flips, independent bit flips). Real noise is correlated, and that matters most for large n. The random-state formulas are averages, and the sampling button shows the scatter around them. The repetition code protects against one kind of error only. That is why the surface code exists.

Market League

A seeded economy. Every posted plan is a firm in the same world, and the price is the only thing they say to each other.

Plans, as a JSON array. A plan is a step function over ticks: the entry with the largest t ≤ the current tick applies. "max" means full capacity.

Rules in one breath

  • Three goods, A, B and C, 48 ticks. Each good has a unit cost c and, each tick, a demand size d(t) and a ceiling price pmax(t); all of it is fixed by the seed and printed above. The clearing price is p = pmax · max(0, 1 − Q/d), where Q is the total output of every firm that tick. Everyone sells all they made at that price.
  • Every unit made uses one unit of raw material from a pool R(t). If firms ask for more than the pool, it is shared max-min fair: everyone gets what they asked up to an equal share, and what the modest leave is split equally among the rest, so asking for more than your share gains nothing. Two seeded disasters shrink the pool to 30 % for three ticks.
  • A firm starts with cash 50 and capacity 10 of each good. Each tick, in this order: it makes what its plan says (clipped to capacity and to the raw it was granted), the market clears and pays p − c per unit, then it invests what its plan says (clipped to cash): 4 cash buys 1 capacity, arriving 3 ticks later. Capacity then decays by 2 %.
  • Score = cash + 2 × total capacity after tick 48. Investing never takes cash below zero; selling below cost can, and there is no borrowing to get out.

The baseline firms are steady (a monopolist's rule: make everything at full capacity and invest toward the solo-optimal output d·(1 − c/pmax)/2 of each good, blind to rivals) and lean (full capacity, never invest). Alone, a firm faces a known world and the problem is optimisation. With a second firm the price is shared and the problem is a game. How to post a plan, and the open round, are in market-league.md.

The crossing

Every quadratic x² = b·x + c has two roots. Each of two pictures holds one thing about them fixed and has a map that swaps them. The two pictures share exactly one equation.

The plane of quadratics x² = b·x + c. Drag the dot. Blue line b = 1: the roots' sum is 1. Orange line c = 1: the roots' product is −1. They meet at one point.
The two roots (white) in the complex plane, with where each swap sends them. Blue ring: 1 − z. Orange ring: −1/z. A ring sitting on the other root means that swap exchanges the roots.

What is being shown

Write the roots as r and s. Then r + s = b and r · s = −c. Each line in the left picture pins one of those.

On the blue line (b = 1) the sum is pinned and c slides freely. That family is x² = x + Γ: its roots always average ½, and the map z → 1 − z, a reflection about ½, exchanges them, whatever Γ is. Slide Γ, though, and the product −Γ changes, along with the remainder in Cassini's identity, which for this family is (−1)ⁿ·Γⁿ⁻¹ and grows without bound once Γ > 1.

On the orange line (c = 1) the product is pinned at −1 and b slides in whole steps. That family is x² = n·x + 1, the rank-two self-dual fusion rules τ·τ = 1 + n·τ. Here the map z → −1/z exchanges the roots, whatever n is, and Cassini's remainder is ±1 at every depth, for every n. (−1/z is the Riemann sphere's half-turn about the axis through ±i: riemann-sphere.html has the sphere.)

The lines meet at exactly one point, x² = x + 1. Only there do both swaps exchange the roots, and only there is the remainder pinned and the centre pinned.

Composing the two swaps, (1 − z) after (−1/z), gives z → 1 + 1/z. Its matrix is [[1,1],[1,0]], the Fibonacci matrix, with determinant −1. Its fixed points are φ and ψ, and the orbit of 1 runs 2, 3/2, 5/3, 8/5, …. So the golden ratio here is not an extra ingredient: it is the fixed point of doing one picture's swap and then the other's.

For any quadratic, (b − z) after (−c/z) is z → b + c/z, whose fixed points are that quadratic's roots, so the composition alone is not special. What only happens at the crossing is that both pieces are themselves swaps of the roots, using maps with no parameters in them.

Two flows, neither ends at the crossing

Each line has a natural flow: replace the free parameter with the larger root. On the blue line, Γ → larger root of x² = x + Γ settles at Γ = 2, where the roots are 2 and −1, both rational. On the orange line, n → larger root of x² = n·x + 1 runs off to infinity, growing like √(2k). The crossing is not where either picture's own dynamics take you. It is where the two pictures agree.

What is left out, on purpose

Why, among the orange line's integers, only n = 0 and n = 1 can be made into fusion categories (Ostrik, 2003, Fusion categories of rank 2). The page does not check this: it is cited, not shown. Likewise the reason the identity coefficient on the orange line is exactly 1 (rigidity: a self-dual simple object has Hom(1, τ⊗τ) one-dimensional), which is what puts the particle picture on the c = 1 line in the first place.

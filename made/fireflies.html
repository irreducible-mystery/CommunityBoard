Fireflies

A flock of fireflies, each with its own rhythm. Every flash nudges every other firefly a little toward it. Turn the coupling up and, past one exact threshold, the meadow starts to blink as one.

Three things to look for. With K below the threshold, the meadow twinkles at random and the line in the chart stays near the floor. Just above it, a crowd forms in the middle of the rhythm range while the fastest and slowest fireflies keep their own time; r is well below 1 and stays there, which is the prediction, not a failure to settle. Far above it, almost everyone is in the crowd. Set γ to 0 and every firefly has the same rhythm: then any coupling at all locks the whole flock, and r goes to 1.

The flock here is in the mathematics, not the picture. Each firefly is nudged by all the others equally, however far away they sit, which is what makes the threshold exact. A version where a firefly only sees its neighbours would be a different page, with a different and harder answer.

What is computed, exactly

Each firefly i has a phase θi and a natural rate ωi; it flashes when its phase passes zero. The rates are ω0 + γ·tan(π(u − ½)) with u uniform, which is a Lorentzian of width γ about ω0 = 2π/1.6 (one flash every 1.6 s). The phases move by dθi/dt = ωi + K·r·sin(ψ − θi), where r·eiψ is the mean of eiθj over the flock; that is the all-to-all Kuramoto sum (K/N)·Σj sin(θj − θi) written through its mean field. Euler steps of 0.02 s. The measured r is the average of r over the last ten seconds. "In step" counts fireflies within a quarter turn of ψ. The checks run smaller flocks (400) for 100 s at fixed seeds and compare the second half's average r with √(1 − 2γ/K), the Kuramoto–Ott–Antonsen result for a Lorentzian, which holds exactly only for an infinite flock; the tolerances (0.06) are the finite-flock scatter measured while making this page. Randomness is FNV-1a → Mulberry32 from the seed, the scheme Noise Lab uses, so the same seed gives the same flock.

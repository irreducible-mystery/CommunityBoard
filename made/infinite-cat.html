The infinite cat

The end of the series, after zero and approaching infinity (separate pull requests; the links work once they are merged). Nothing here leaves the page.

Take n qubits in the cat state (|0…0⟩ + |1…1⟩)/√2, the both corner at its most extreme. Next to it put a coin: a box that is all-0 or all-1 with even odds, with no superposition at all. This page asks a single question: which measurements can tell the two apart? As n grows the answer shrinks, and at infinity it is none that touch only finitely many qubits.

1. Every part agrees

The cat's density matrix is the coin's plus two corner entries, ½|0…0⟩⟨1…1| and its mirror. Tracing out even a single qubit erases both entries, so every proper part of the cat is exactly the coin's part. Any question about at most n−1 qubits gets the same answer from both.

Questions on qubits are products of I, X, Y, Z. Of all 4n of them, the page finds the ones on which cat and coin disagree: exactly 2n−1, each with X or Y on every qubit and an even number of Y's. The fraction is 2n−1/4n, which goes to zero. Yet the two states are far from identical: their trace distance is ½ for every n. The difference is real but it lives nowhere in particular.

2. The one question fades

Measure X on every qubit and multiply the ±1 outcomes. For the cat the product is +1 on every shot. For the coin it is a fair coin flip. That is the whole difference. If each readout is wrong with probability e, every error flips the product, so the cat's average falls to (1−2e)n. Telling cat from coin at 3 standard deviations takes about (3/(1−2e)n)2 shots, which grows exponentially in n.

Mean product of all n X outcomes. Line: (1−2e)n. Orange: simulated cat. Grey: simulated coin.

3. At infinity: two worlds, no bridge

Now let n be infinite. Any measurement anyone can actually make involves finitely many qubits, or is a limit of such measurements: these are the quasi-local observables. Section 1 showed that on every finite set of qubits the cat and the coin agree exactly. So on every quasi-local observable the infinite cat and the coin give identical statistics. For every physical purpose they are the same state.

The mathematics confirms this in a stronger form. All-0 and all-1 now live in different superselection sectors: no quasi-local operator has a nonzero matrix element between them, because getting from one to the other means flipping infinitely many qubits. A "superposition" across sectors is a mixture with extra notation. In the algebraic language, the two pure states give inequivalent representations, and the cat's state is the mixture of them (Bratteli and Robinson, Operator Algebras and Quantum Statistical Mechanics; the same mechanism underlies spontaneous symmetry breaking). At infinity, both becomes either.

4. The same fact, put to work

The surface code uses this deliberately. Its stored answer can only be flipped by an operator that stretches from edge to edge, d qubits at least. Every lighter operator either sets off a check or does nothing to the answer. The page proves it by brute force: it lists every X-type operator of each weight w ≤ d, keeps the ones that set off no check ("silent"), and counts how many of those flip the answer.

Below d, every silent operator is a product of checks and the answer is untouched. At w = d the first answer-flippers appear. Every one of them crosses the grid from top to bottom, touching each row exactly once. Only d of them are straight columns; the rest zig-zag (8 of them at d = 3, of which 3 are straight). As d → ∞, no finite action can reach the stored answer. The same fact that turns the cat into a coin makes the code's answer permanent: what no finite part holds, no finite act can change.

What this is not

A finite page cannot hold infinitely many qubits. Sections 1, 2 and 4 are exact finite computations whose pattern is proved in general. Section 3 is the standard theorem those patterns point to, stated here and not computed. "Every measurement is quasi-local" is a statement about what is physical. A mathematician can write down a global observable that distinguishes the two states, but it has no finite approximation that does. Real cats are not GHZ states. Decoherence picks out sectors in a messier, approximate way. The surface code census uses only X-type operators; Z-type is the same by symmetry.

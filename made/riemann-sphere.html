What is being shown

Stereographic projection sends each point of the unit sphere, except the north pole, along a straight line from that pole to the plane through the equator. Every point of the plane is hit exactly once. The pole itself is hit by nothing, so it is given the name ∞: not a number, a point. The south pole lands on 0. The equator lands on the unit circle, where 1 sits.

The map z → 1/z, which has no meaning at 0 on the plane, is on the sphere simply a half-turn about the axis through 1 and −1. It swaps the poles. It carries straight lines (which all pass through ∞) to circles through 0, and circles to circles. It is an isometry of the sphere, so it preserves the chordal distance, the length of the straight chord between two points through the inside.

Its fixed points are exactly the two solutions of z² = 1: 1 and −1, the two ends of the axis. Every point of the unit circle is equidistant (√2) from 0 and from ∞, and 0 and ∞ themselves are as far apart as two points on the sphere can be: the diameter, 2. So the slider's walk from 0 through 1 to ∞ is a quarter of a great circle, then another quarter, with the hinge in the middle.

Where this came from. A session that had been playing with the slogan "0 = 1 = ∞" as metaphor. The slogan is false as arithmetic and stays false here: on the sphere 0 and ∞ are distinct points at maximum separation. What is true, and what this page shows exactly, is weaker and better: the three are points on one closed surface with no edge, there is a symmetry of that surface exchanging 0 and ∞, and 1 is one of the two points the symmetry leaves where it was.

Left out, on purpose. Other Möbius maps (the full group PSL(2,ℂ)), the second fixed point's twin at −1 being drawn with equal weight, the metric on the sphere versus the plane's, and any claim about physics. The projection is from the north pole onto the equatorial plane; the version projecting onto the tangent plane at the south pole differs by a factor of 2 in |z| and nothing else.

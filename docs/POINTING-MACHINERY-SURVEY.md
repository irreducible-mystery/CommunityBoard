# Pointing Machinery — Traditions, Methods, Institutions and Formal Tools That Point At the Frame Itself

## Summary

**Question.** What real-world traditions, methods, institutions and formal tools have as their *primary* function not arguing inside a framework, but (1) naming assumed “floors” — constructed distinctions treated as fundamental; (2) round-trip translation between two vocabularies/models, treating the residue that fails to translate as the finding; (3) “stranger tests” — upgrade only after a fresh, blind, stake-free evaluator judges two formulations to be the same claim; (4) triangulation — truth as what survives translation through the most independent frames; and (5) hunting invariants across scale/description vs. frame-local artefacts?

**Short answer.** No single tradition or institution found performs the combined function. The pieces exist, scattered, and each piece is strongest exactly where the others are weakest:

- **For naming floors as a repeatable habit**, the strongest secular machinery is Lakatos’s hard-core / protective-belt distinction, Argyris & Schön’s double-loop learning, and Heuer’s ACH assumption-listing — all descriptive or procedural, none with strong outcome evidence.
- **For round-trip translation and residue**, the strongest *formal* machinery is applied category theory — Spivak’s ologs and functorial data migration — and, descriptively, Galison’s trading zones and Star & Griesemer’s boundary objects. Category theory is the only candidate where “what fails to translate” can be made a precise, checkable object (a functor that does not exist, a diagram that does not commute, data that cannot be migrated without loss). Galison/Star describe translation in the wild but provide no test.
- **For stranger tests**, the closest real practices are **blind analysis in particle physics, double-blind peer review, and independent replication (Open Science Collaboration, Many Labs)** — not the mission’s semantic-identity version. None of them, as practised, asks a context-free evaluator, blind to provenance, “are these two formulations the same claim?” as a gate for upgrading a claim. That specific gate appears to be a **gap** (see Synthesis).
- **For triangulation / invariants**, the strongest exemplars are the **Erlangen program** (a geometry *is* its invariants under a transformation group) and the **2019 SI redefinition** (units anchored to fixed constants of nature, realisable anywhere, by anyone, without the artefact). Perspectival realism (Giere, Massimi) is the philosophical version: validate by cross-perspectival comparison, accept no “view from nowhere.”
- **For equipollent opposition as a discipline**, Prāsaṅgika Madhyamaka and Pyrrhonian skepticism are the purest historical forms — and both deliberately refuse to convert the pointing into a new position, which is both their power and why they produce no cumulative institution.

**Evidence warning that runs through the whole survey.** Several of the most-cited secular techniques have *weak or negative* experimental evidence despite wide institutional adoption: ACH in particular (multiple experiments, little/no benefit, possible harm), “consider the opposite” (small, mixed effects), and the Hong–Page “diversity trumps ability” theorem (the *prediction* theorem is an algebraic identity; the *trumps-ability* theorem is widely argued to build its conclusion into its premises). Conversely, the practices with the best measured outcomes — forecasting tournaments with scoring, aggregation and recalibration; Registered Reports; blind analysis — work by *changing incentives and timing* (score kept, design judged before results are known, answer hidden while method is fixed), not by exhorting people to notice their assumptions.

## Findings

### 1. Contemplative / philosophical traditions

#### 1.1 Dzogchen pointing-out instruction (*ngo sprod*) and Mahāmudrā pointing-out

- **What it is.** In Dzogchen, *rig pa'i ngo sprod* — “introduction to awareness”; in Mahāmudrā, *sems kyi ngo sprod* — “pointing out the nature of mind.” A lama introduces the student directly to the nature of their own mind (*rigpa*, awareness; “ordinary mind” in Mahāmudrā), traditionally as the culminating / fourth empowerment. Garab Dorje’s “Three Statements” summarise the path: (1) introduce directly the face of rigpa itself, (2) decide upon one thing only, (3) confidence in liberation.
- **Mechanism, in practice.** Not an argument and not information transfer. The teacher contrives a situation / instruction intended to make the student *recognise* what is already present but overlooked because conceptual elaboration covers it — then the student must confirm recognition, and stabilise it. The pointing succeeds or fails in first person; the teacher’s role is ostensive, like pointing at something in the room.
- **What it can surface.** The most basic “floor” of all in this frame: the distinction subject/object, and the assumption that mind is its contents. It is the literal archetype of the mission’s “pointing at the frame.”
- **What it cannot surface.** Anything third-person or cumulative. Recognition is not transferable, not scored, and not separable from teacher authority and lineage — which is itself an un-pointed-at floor inside the tradition (Kapstein, cited in the source, notes the historically contested, partly Chan-derived origin that the tradition was motivated not to acknowledge). There is no procedure by which a failed or counterfeit pointing-out can be distinguished from a successful one by a stranger.
- **Evidence.** No controlled evidence for the specific epistemic claim is possible on the tradition’s own terms; scholarly evidence is historical/textual only. Treat devotional descriptions as primary-source evidence of *what the practice claims to be*, not of efficacy.
- **Sources.** Pointing-out instruction, terminology, Garab Dorje’s three statements, Kapstein on Chan influence — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointing-out_instruction (`index`; tertiary, but citing Kapstein and lineage texts) and the MDPI *Encyclopedia* entry https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/history/show/83419 (`index`).

#### 1.2 Zen “direct pointing” and the finger / moon trope

- **What it is.** The four-line stanza attributed (in the 12th century, centuries later) to Bodhidharma: “A special transmission outside the scriptures / Not founded upon words and letters / By pointing directly to [one’s] mind / It lets one see into [one’s own true] nature and [thus] attain Buddhahood.” The finger-pointing-at-the-moon image is older: in the *Śūraṅgama Sūtra* (and a parallel in the *Laṅkāvatāra*), one guided by the finger should see the moon; “if he looks at the finger instead and mistakes it for the moon, he loses not only the moon but the finger also.”
- **Mechanism.** Shock techniques (Mazu, Linji), kōan, shouting, blows, burning one’s commentaries (Teshan/Tokusan) — interventions designed to break identification of the *guide* (text, concept, teacher) with the *guided-to*. In modern terms: a trained refusal to confuse map-level and territory-level, enforced behaviourally rather than argued.
- **Can / cannot.** Can surface attachment to formulations *in the moment, in a person*. Cannot produce a public, revisable record of *which* assumption was surfaced, and — as the scholarly source itself warns — the slogan “do not establish words and letters” was historically *not* a denial of texts but a warning about confusing a guide with truth; the pop version (“Zen rejects concepts”) is a distortion. The tradition also generated vast scriptural / kōan literature — a self-illustrating failure mode: pointing machinery congeals into a new floor.
- **Evidence.** Historical/philological (the stanza’s late attribution is itself documented). No outcome evidence separable from the tradition’s soteriological claims.
- **Sources.** Zen scriptures, transmission stanza, “not a denial of the recorded words… but a warning to those who… mistook [teaching as guide] for the truth itself” — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_scriptures (`index`); EBSCO research starter quoting Dumoulin, “like a finger pointing at the moon” — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/bodhidharma (`index`); *Śūraṅgama* passage in a San Francisco Zen Center talk transcript — https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/surangama-sutra-class-0 (`index`; practice source).

#### 1.3 Nāgārjuna’s Madhyamaka — *prasaṅga*

- **What it is.** Nāgārjuna (c. 2nd c. CE), *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* (MMK): all phenomena are empty (*śūnya*) of inherent existence (*svabhāva*) because they arise dependently. In the *Vigrahavyāvartanī* v.29 he states the method’s signature: “If I had any thesis (*pratijñā*), that fault would apply to me. But I do not have any thesis, so there is indeed no fault for me.” MMK XIII.8: emptiness is “the means of extinction / expulsion of all views (*sarva-dṛṣṭīnāṃ niḥsaraṇa*)” — including, if reified, the view of emptiness.
- **Mechanism.** *Prasaṅga* = consequence / reductio using *only the opponent’s premises*. Buddhapālita’s commentary refuted positions solely by showing their unacceptable consequences; the later Prāsaṅgika / Svātantrika split (Buddhapālita & Candrakīrti vs. Bhāvaviveka) is precisely a dispute over whether the Mādhyamika may also advance independent (*svatantra*) arguments of his own, or must assert none. The Daktsang summary in the scholarly source: Prāsaṅgikas “qua Mādhyamikas, make no assertions and only engage in debate… by deconstructing their theses by means of reductio analyses. They put forward no positive theses of their own, and their philosophical work is complete when opponents realise the flaws of their positions and abandon them.”
- **Can / cannot.** This is the purest historical form of *frame-pointing without installing a rival frame*: it surfaces the opponent’s load-bearing assumption (inherent existence) by deriving absurdity from it, and it is self-applying (the ladder must not become a floor). It cannot — by design — adjudicate between two positive models, build a measurement, or accumulate results; and interpreters disagree fundamentally on whether it is coherent (Garfield’s rational / coherentist reconstruction vs. Huntington’s “Madhyamaka trick” / soteriological *upāya* reading, both documented in the source). That disagreement is data: a method that asserts nothing leaves its successors nothing shared to test.
- **Evidence.** Textual and commentarial, 1,800 years of it; the “evidence” is the method’s survival and formal interest, not experimental efficacy.
- **Sources.** *Vigrahavyāvartanī* 29 and MMK XIII.8 in scholarly discussion — https://djsengal.substack.com/p/thesis-on-analysis-nagarjunas-mulamadhyamakakari-e67 (`index`; scholarly blog, quoting the verses) and https://www.researchgate.net/publication/290229207_The_Dispeller_of_Disputes_Nagarjuna's_Vigrahavyavartani (`index`); Prāsaṅgika anti-foundationalism, Daktsang — https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/12/11/991 (`index`, peer-reviewed); Garfield’s translation *The Fundamental Wisdom of the Middle Way* (OUP, 1995) as the standard English primary text — https://books.google.al/books?id=XylwAgAAQBAJ&source=gbs_book_other_versions_r&cad=3 (`index`).

#### 1.4 Pyrrhonian skepticism — equipollence (*isostheneia*)

- **What it is.** Sextus Empiricus, *Outlines of Pyrrhonism* (*PH*) 1.8: skepticism is “an ability to set up oppositions among things that appear and things that are thought of in any way whatsoever, an ability from which, because of the equipollence (*isostheneia*) in the opposed objects and arguments, we come first to suspension of judgement (*epochē*) and then to tranquillity (*ataraxia*).” *PH* 1.10: equipollence is “equality in respect of credibility and lack of credibility, so that none of the conflicting arguments takes precedence over any other as more credible.”
- **Mechanism.** A *repertoire for generating* oppositions on demand: the Ten Modes (attributed to Aenesidemus) — differences among animals, among humans, among senses, in circumstances, positions, mixtures, quantities, relations, frequency, customs — plus Agrippa’s modes (regress, circularity, hypothesis, relativity, dispute). For any dogmatic claim, produce a counter-consideration of equal force keyed to the judger, the judged, or both. Live by appearances, laws and customs, without assent to how things are in themselves.
- **Can / cannot.** Uniquely, this is a *portable generator*, not a doctrine: it can be aimed at any frame, including (self-consistently) its own slogans, which Sextus says purge themselves like a laxative. It cannot rank frames, detect which opposition is merely verbal, or tell you what to do when a decision is forced and equipollence is manufactured by a bad-faith opponent — the modern “both-sides” failure mode is Pyrrhonism without its therapeutic intent and its honesty about appearances.
- **Evidence.** None experimental; modern value is as a discipline of *withholding* status-upgrade, i.e. the negative half of the mission’s stranger test.
- **Sources.** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Pyrrho” and “Ancient Skepticism” — https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/win2024/entries/pyrrho/ and https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/spr2010/entries/skepticism-ancient/ (`index`, scholarly); *PH* 1.8 / 1.10 definitions quoted in https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335579856_Does_Pyrrhonism_Have_Practical_or_Epistemic_Value (`index`).

#### 1.5 Wittgenstein — ladder, showing vs. saying

- **What it is.** *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* 4.121 (logical form cannot be represented, it “mirrors itself” / shows itself), 6.54: “My propositions serve as elucidations in this way: anyone who understands me eventually recognises them as nonsensical, when he has used them — as steps — to climb up beyond them. (He must, so to speak, throw away the ladder after he has climbed up it.) He must transcend these propositions, and then he will see the world aright.” 7: “Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent.”
- **Mechanism.** A text that *uses* a frame to display the frame’s limit, then instructs the reader to discard the text. The resolute reading (Diamond, Conant) — documented in the source — distinguishes the book’s *frame* (preface, 6.53–6.54, etc., meant seriously as instructions for how to treat the book) from its *body* (to be recognised as illuminating nonsense).
- **Can / cannot.** It names, better than anything else in §1, the *self-reference problem* every pointing practice faces: the pointing is itself made of the stuff pointed at. It provides no repeatable procedure, no inter-rater test, and its central distinction is contested (ineffable-truths reading vs. resolute reading) — i.e. specialists cannot agree on whether the ladder shows something or nothing. That is itself an instructive datum for any program that relies on “showing.”
- **Sources.** 6.54 text and saying/showing analysis — https://www.petemandik.com/philosophy/papers/witt.html (`index`); elucidatory / resolute reading, frame vs. body — https://wab.uib.no/agora/tools/alws/collection-3-issue-1-article-23.annotate (`index`, Wittgenstein Archives, Bergen).

#### 1.6 Korzybski — general semantics, “the map is not the territory”

- **What it is.** Alfred Korzybski, *Science and Sanity* (1933); Institute of General Semantics founded 1938 (still operating). Dictum, p.58: “A map is not the territory it represents, but, if correct, it has a *similar structure* to the territory, which accounts for its usefulness.” If ideally correct it would contain “the map of the map; the map of the map of the map; and so on, endlessly” — self-reflexiveness of language. Supporting machinery: levels of abstraction and the **structural differential** (a physical diagram / device for training awareness that sensing omits characteristics, description omits more, inference more still); extensional devices (indexing — cow₁ ≠ cow₂; dating; quotation marks; hyphens) intended as *habits*, not theses.
- **Mechanism.** Perceptual training, closest in §1 to the mission’s “repeatable perceptual habit of naming floors”: pause at the silent (non-verbal) level, index and date your terms, notice which abstraction level a statement lives on.
- **Can / cannot.** Can surface level-confusion (treating an inference as an observation, a label as the thing) in everyday and organisational talk — the part popularised by Hayakawa, Wendell Johnson and others into communications curricula. Cannot validate its own neurological / therapeutic claims: Korzybski presented it as empirical science; the documented verdict is harsh — Max Black: “some hypothetical neurology fortified with dogmatic metaphysics”; Martin Gardner (1952): the work “moves into the realm of cultism and pseudo-science”; a majority-of-psychiatrists judgement (cited in the source) that it is not an effective treatment. Flag: the *slogan* has been completely absorbed into common sense; the *system* has not survived scientific scrutiny. That split is the lesson.
- **Sources.** *Science and Sanity*, dictum, structural differential, three premises (map ≠ territory; no map covers all; maps are self-reflexive) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_Sanity and https://www.thefreelibrary.com/General+semantics%3a+understanding+Korzybski%27s+formulations.-a0347004725 (`index`); criticism, Institute founded 1938 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Semantics (`index`); Korzybski 1933 p.58 quote via Institute of General Semantics event page — https://time-binding.org/event-4967707 (`index`).

### 2. Philosophy & sociology of science

#### 2.1 Galison — trading zones, pidgins and creoles

- **What it is.** Peter Galison, *Image and Logic: A Material Culture of Microphysics* (1997). Against Kuhn’s picture of incommensurable “island empires, each under the rule of its own system of validation,” Galison shows theorists, experimentalists (image vs. logic traditions) and instrument-makers coordinating through **trading zones**: local sites where groups who assign *utterly different significance* to the objects traded can still exchange them, developing restricted **jargons → pidgins** (work languages, no one’s mother tongue) → sometimes **creoles** (full interlanguages that become a new subculture’s native tongue — e.g. biochemistry).
- **Mechanism.** Coordination *without* consensus on meaning. The traded term is deliberately thinned — a “skeleton concept” whose local meaning inside the zone is simplified relative to its meaning inside each home culture — and coordination of *action and belief* stabilises anyway. Diachronicity, locality and contextuality are the analytic handles.
- **Can / cannot.** This is the best *descriptive* account of the mission’s round-trip translation: it predicts exactly where residue will appear (in what each side privately means by the traded term) and why work can succeed despite it. It is not a *test*: Galison gives the historian a lens, not the participants a protocol, and critics (Staley, documented in the source) argue some celebrated cases needed no pidgin at all because argumentative overlap was already sufficient — i.e. the lens can over-diagnose incommensurability. No outcome metric is offered.
- **Sources.** Galison via review and scholarly discussion — https://www.academia.edu/30439797/Review_of_Galison_Image_and_Logic_A_Material_Culture_of_Microphysics (`index`); Staley critique — https://rational-action.com/etherwave/2009/06/14/watch-your-language-pt-2-galison-vs-staley/ (`index`, scholarly blog); quantitative application / limits (supersymmetry trading zone breaking down) — https://philarchive.org/rec/GAUHRP (`index`, peer-reviewed).

#### 2.2 Star & Griesemer — boundary objects

- **What it is.** Star & Griesemer, “Institutional Ecology, ‘Translations’ and Boundary Objects,” *Social Studies of Science* (1989), from Berkeley’s Museum of Vertebrate Zoology (1907–39): amateurs, trappers, patrons, administrators and professional biologists cooperated without sharing aims. **Boundary objects** are “plastic enough to adapt to local needs and the constraints of the several parties employing them, yet robust enough to maintain a common identity across sites… weakly structured in common use, and strongly structured in individual-site use.” Types: repositories, ideal types, coincident-boundary objects, standardised forms.
- **Mechanism.** The *object* (specimen, field note, map, standard form) does the translation work that shared meaning cannot. Each world fills the weakly-structured common form with its own strong structure.
- **Can / cannot.** Directly relevant to triangulation: identity-across-sites is achieved *without* identity-of-meaning, so a boundary object is a working demonstration that “same claim” is ambiguous between same-token and same-content — precisely the ambiguity a stranger test must resolve. Limitation: the concept is so elastic it is now applied to almost anything shared (Star herself later warned against dilution); it diagnoses, it does not discriminate good translations from bad, and it can *conceal* residue — cooperation succeeds, so nobody inspects what each side thinks was agreed.
- **Sources.** Original definition p.393 and typology pp.410–11, quoted extensively — https://www.open.edu/openlearncreate/mod/page/view.php?id=236081 and https://scalar.usc.edu/works/boundary-objects-guide/boundary-objects (`index`, university sources); retrospective in *Nature / Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-03135-w?error=cookies_not_supported&code=6e12fb64-302f-4f6b-96d2-3c2ede9f736d (`index`, peer-reviewed).

#### 2.3 Lakatos — hard core vs. protective belt

- **What it is.** Imre Lakatos, “Falsification and the Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes” (1970); *The Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes* (1978). The unit of appraisal is not a theory but a **research programme**: a **hard core** of assumptions rendered “irrefutable by fiat” (the *negative heuristic* forbids directing *modus tollens* at it), surrounded by a **protective belt** of auxiliary hypotheses that take the hits and are revised under a *positive heuristic*. A programme is *progressive* if revisions predict novel facts, some confirmed; *degenerating* if revisions only patch anomalies after the fact.
- **Mechanism.** An explicit, written partition of a frame into “what we will not question (yet)” vs. “what we will sacrifice first,” plus a *score* for belt-revisions (novel prediction or mere patch?).
- **Can / cannot.** This is the single closest match in all of §2 to “naming assumed floors as a repeatable habit,” because it forces the naming *in advance* and makes the cost visible: every anomaly absorbed by the belt is a receipt. Uranus’s orbit → posit Neptune (progressive belt move) rather than abandon Newtonian core is the canonical good case. Limits: the partition is declared by the programme’s own adherents (no stranger decides what is really core); there is no deadline at which a degenerating programme *must* be abandoned — Lakatos explicitly allows rational persistence — so the machinery names floors without ever compelling anyone to step off one; and rational reconstruction can be applied retrospectively to almost any history.
- **Sources.** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Lakatos” — https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/fall2016/entries/lakatos/ (`index`, scholarly; quoted passages on hard core, protective belt, progressive/degenerating); application / summary — https://science-education-research.com/research-methodology/research-programmes/ (`index`).

#### 2.4 Kuhn — paradigms and incommensurability

- **What it is.** Thomas Kuhn, *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (1962). **Normal science** is puzzle-solving supplied, and bounded, by a **paradigm**; phenomena that will not fit “are often not seen at all,” and failure reflects on the practitioner, not the tools. **Anomalies → crisis → revolution.** Rival paradigms are **incommensurable**: no common measure; proponents have different problems, different standards, different conceptual networks / similarity sets, and in a qualified sense see different worlds.
- **Mechanism.** None, deliberately: Kuhn is diagnostic / historical. His contribution to pointing machinery is the negative demonstration that *within* normal science the frame is invisible by design — training by exemplars (not definitions) installs it below the level where argument could reach it.
- **Can / cannot.** Can explain why floor-naming feels like incompetence or disloyalty from inside, and why translation fails at the level of *what counts as a problem*, not just vocabulary. Cannot, on its own terms, supply the mission’s triangulation criterion: if there is no common measure, “what survives translation” cannot be read off Kuhn — later Kuhn softened incommensurability to local, taxonomic untranslatability, which is the usable version (partial translation, bilinguals, residue localised to a cluster of terms).
- **Sources.** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Thomas Kuhn” — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/thomas-kuhn/ (`index`, scholarly).

#### 2.5 Perspectival realism — Giere, Massimi

- **What it is.** Ronald Giere, *Scientific Perspectivism* (2006): the strongest legitimate claim is conditional — “according to this highly confirmed theory (or reliable instrument), the world seems to be roughly such-and-such”; no unconditional “complete and literally correct picture” is available. Models represent via partial similarity, for an agent, for a purpose. Michela Massimi, *Perspectival Realism* (OUP, 2022): realism without a God’s-eye view; knowledge claims are assessed against perspectival standards of performance-adequacy, and **phenomena** are “stable events… modally robust across a variety of perspectival data-to-phenomena inferences” — i.e. what recurs under many independent routes of inference earns ontological standing.
- **Mechanism.** Cross-perspectival comparison, diachronic (across theory change) and synchronic (across disciplines / cultures / instruments), in place of correspondence checked from nowhere. Elgin’s formulation quoted in the source: representations are objective insofar as they “contain information that is invariant across representations of the same object” and testable in that “information can be accessed from multiple perspectives.”
- **Can / cannot.** This is the philosophical statement closest to the mission’s triangulation axiom, and Massimi’s “modal robustness across perspectival inferences” is its most precise form. Limits: it is a position, not a protocol — it does not specify how many perspectives, how independent they must be, or what to do when robustness is an artefact of shared instruments / shared training (the common-mode failure problem, §6). Critics argue Giere’s version tips into relativism or collapses into ordinary partial realism.
- **Sources.** PhilPapers bibliography / summary of perspectival realism, cross-perspectival validation — https://philpapers.org/browse/perspectival-realism (`index`, scholarly); Massimi 2022 discussion, phenomena as modally robust — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-025-05222-0 and Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews — https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/perspectival-realism/ (`index`, peer-reviewed / scholarly review); Giere conditional-claim formulation — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7297829/ (`index`, peer-reviewed).

#### 2.6 Second-order cybernetics — von Foerster

- **What it is.** Heinz von Foerster (1974, *Cybernetics of Cybernetics*): first-order cybernetics = cybernetics of **observed** systems; second-order = cybernetics of **observing** systems — “the control of control and the communication of communication.” The observer is inside the domain, responsible for the distinctions drawn; allied to radical constructivism (von Glasersfeld), autopoiesis (Maturana & Varela), and Margaret Mead’s reflexive turn at the Macy conferences.
- **Mechanism.** Recursive application: apply the theory of systems to the system doing the theorising; make the observer’s distinctions (system boundary, input/output partition) explicit objects of study; von Foerster’s *eigenforms* — stable objects as fixed points of recursive operations.
- **Can / cannot.** Supplies the *formal intuition* behind invariants in §4: an “object” is what stays stable under repeated application of an operation — invariance as a definition of reality, not a discovery about it. Practically, second-order cybernetics generated therapy, design and action-research practices but no agreed test for when reflexivity has gone deep enough vs. become an infinite regress or a licence for subjectivism — von Foerster himself framed the exo/endo choice as epistemological *and ethical*, i.e. not decidable from inside either mode.
- **Sources.** Definition, observed vs. observing systems — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-order_cybernetics (`index`; well-referenced tertiary) and scholarly treatment https://www.researchgate.net/publication/306059358_Second-Order_Cybernetics_as_a_Fundamental_Revolution_in_Science (`index`).

### 3. Structured verification practices

#### 3.1 Heuer — Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) and structured analytic techniques

- **What it is.** Richards J. Heuer Jr. (45-year CIA veteran), *Psychology of Intelligence Analysis* (CIA Center for the Study of Intelligence, 1999), ch.8. ACH is an 8-step procedure “grounded in basic insights from cognitive psychology, decision analysis, and the scientific method”: (1) identify all possible hypotheses, preferably brainstormed by analysts with different perspectives; (2) list significant evidence *and arguments — including assumptions and logical deductions* — for and against each; (3) build a hypotheses × evidence matrix and assess **diagnosticity** (evidence that fits all hypotheses diagnoses nothing); (4) refine / delete; (5) draw tentative conclusions by *disconfirmation* — the least-inconsistent hypothesis wins, not the best-supported; (6) test sensitivity to critical evidence / assumptions; (7) report all conclusions with relative likelihoods; (8) identify indicators / milestones to monitor.
- **Mechanism.** Externalisation + forced disconfirmation. The matrix makes the analyst’s frame inspectable by others and creates the audit trail Heuer prized for controversial issues.
- **Can / cannot.** Can surface: assumptions *that the analyst can already articulate when prompted to list them as “evidence”*, and evidence whose non-diagnosticity is invisible in prose. Cannot surface: a hypothesis nobody in the room generated (step 1 is the whole game, and it is unprotected), a shared assumption common to *all* listed hypotheses (the matrix compares columns; a row-level / frame-level assumption built into every column is invisible), or deception designed to be consistent.
- **Evidence — negative, and important.** A critical review identified seven articles describing six experiments testing ACH: “ACH — as a whole — has little to no overall benefit on judgment quality, and may even harm it… We consequently discourage intelligence organizations from mandating the training or use of ACH.” Dhami, Belton & Mandel: ACH-trained analysts did not follow all the steps; mixed evidence on confirmation bias; ACH “may increase judgment inconsistency and error.” Mandel, Karvetski & Dhami (2018), with real intelligence analysts: the ACH group assessed information usefulness better but the *control group was a little more accurate and coherent*; by contrast **recalibration + aggregation decreased mean absolute error by 61%**. A 2024 experimental study found the ACH-style matrix orientation itself conferred no benefit, while a transposed presentation did reduce confirmation bias — suggesting any benefit lies in generic structured comparison, not ACH as specified.
- **Sources.** Heuer ch.8 primary text — https://www.allworldwars.com/Psychology-of-Intelligence-Analysis.html (`index`); critical review — https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370682606_Critical_Review_of_the_Analysis_of_Competing_Hypotheses_Technique_Lessons_for_the_Intelligence_Community (`index`, forthcoming in *Intelligence and National Security*); Mandel et al. 2018 abstract — https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2019-02876-009 (`index`); matrix-orientation experiment — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11169332/ (`index`, peer-reviewed).

#### 3.2 Adversarial collaboration (Kahneman)

- **What it is.** Opponents co-design, *in advance*, a study / analysis that both agree could distinguish their positions, state in writing what each expects and what would change their mind, run it jointly (often with a neutral **arbiter**), and co-author the report — each side may add its own interpretation. Kahneman’s stated aim: replace “angry science” with joint truth-seeking. Canonical case: Kahneman & Klein (2009), “Conditions for Intuitive Expertise: A Failure to Disagree,” *American Psychologist* 64(6) — a heuristics-and-biases sceptic and a naturalistic-decision-making defender converged not on “intuition: yes/no” but on **boundary conditions**: intuition is trustworthy only where (a) the environment is regular enough to have learnable cue–outcome relations and (b) the judge has had prolonged practice with fast, clear feedback.
- **Mechanism.** Pre-commitment + arbiter + rewriting a binary dispute as a specification of the conditions under which each side’s examples arise. The frame-pointing happens in the design negotiation: to agree a discriminating test, each side must state its claim in a form the other can operate.
- **Can / cannot.** Can convert identity-level disagreement into a testable scope condition, and produces a joint artefact neither side could disown. Cannot guarantee minds change — Kahneman himself, in his Edge lecture, is bleak: expect “few minds changing,” expect adversaries to “renege on their commitments to critical experiments… in good faith.” It requires both parties to volunteer, respect each other, and share a goal — conditions that select for exactly the disputes that least need it. It also tests *empirical* claims; it has no purchase where the disagreement is that two formulations are / are not the same claim.
- **Sources.** Kahneman, “Adversarial Collaboration: An EDGE Lecture” (primary) — https://www.edge.org/adversarial-collaboration-daniel-kahneman?ref=refind (`index`); protocol summary (arbiter, advance expectations) — http://www.Lesswrong.com/w/adversarial-collaboration-dispute-protocol (`index`; community source, accurate to Kahneman / Mellers et al.); Kahneman & Klein boundary-conditions case — https://github-wiki-see.page/m/davidamitchell/Research/wiki/2026-03-10-adversarial-agents-shared-goals-multi-perspective (`index`; secondary wiki, citing *American Psychologist* 2009 — verify in the journal before operational use).

#### 3.3 Red teaming

- **What it is.** A chartered, *independent* group that adopts an adversary’s / competitor’s perspective — simulations, vulnerability probes, alternative analysis (Micah Zenko, *Red Team*, 2015, from 200+ interviews). Lineage: Prussian *Kriegsspiel*, devil’s advocate (*advocatus diaboli*), post-9/11 formalisation in US military / intelligence. US Joint Doctrine Note 1-16, *Command Red Team* (2016): red teams “help commanders… think critically and creatively; **challenge assumptions; mitigate groupthink**… and increase opportunities by helping the staff see situations… from alternative perspectives,” and — crucially — red teams “should be **organizationally, physically, and intellectually separate** from the intelligence function… products… reviewed and approved through different product approval chains,” so they are not “shaped by the same institutional factors.”
- **Mechanism.** Licensed, role-protected dissent by *simulation*: the red team does not argue that the plan is wrong, it *enacts* the world in which the plan’s assumptions are false / exploited.
- **Can / cannot.** Can surface assumptions that are socially unsayable by insiders (the plan’s author is in the room) and operational vulnerabilities. Cannot surface assumptions the red team shares with the blue team (same recruitment pool, same clearances, same culture — Zenko’s own cases show red teams being ignored, scoped to exclude the real vulnerability, or commissioned after the decision). Evidence is case-based, not experimental: documented successes and documented failures-to-be-heard; there is no controlled literature showing red-teamed decisions outperform matched controls. The independence requirement in doctrine is an admission that the default state is capture.
- **Sources.** Joint Doctrine Note via Federation of American Scientists — https://fas.org/publication/red-teams/ (`index`); Zenko definition and review — http://ndupress.ndu.edu/Publications/Article/1038854/red-team/ (`index`, National Defense University Press); scholarly definition / history (Ackerman & Clifford) — https://pdf.stafforini.com/ackerman-2021-red-teaming-crisis.pdf (`index`, peer-reviewed).

#### 3.4 Tetlock — Good Judgment Project, superforecasting, calibration

- **What it is.** IARPA’s Aggregative Contingent Estimation (ACE) tournament (2011–15): five competing research programmes, 500+ geopolitical questions, >1 million probabilistic forecasts, scored by Brier score. Philip Tetlock & Barbara Mellers’s **Good Judgment Project (GJP)** won decisively — its best aggregation algorithms were on the right side of 50/50 on 86.2% of daily forecasts, outperforming a working-alone no-training control by ~60% and other teams by ~40% (Tetlock & Gardner, *Superforecasting*, 2015; Mellers et al.). The experimentally isolated drivers (randomised factorial designs inside the tournament): (a) recruitment / retention of better forecasters, (b) **training** — a ~1-hour probabilistic-reasoning / debiasing module gave a durable lift, (c) **teaming**, (d) **aggregation with extremisation** (push the crowd average away from 50% to correct for shared-information double-counting and individual timidity). “Superforecasters” (top ~2%) beat intelligence analysts with access to classified material — by ~30% in Tetlock’s oft-quoted comparison.
- **Mechanism.** Not assumption-discussion: **keeping score**. Frequent granular probability estimates, rapid outcome feedback, Brier scoring, and aggregation turn a frame dispute into a calibration record. Superforecasters’ documented habits (“fox” style: break problems down — *Fermi-ise*; take the outside view first; treat beliefs as hypotheses to update in small increments; look for the clashing causal forces) are behaviours the *scoring regime* selects for, not a curriculum that reliably installs them.
- **Can / cannot.** Can detect, publicly and quantitatively, whose frame is miscalibrated and by how much, on *resolvable, short-to-medium-horizon, well-specified* questions. Cannot score a claim that has no resolution date or agreed resolution criteria — which includes most frame-level claims (“these two theories are equivalent,” “this distinction is artificial”). Tournament questions must be written so a stranger could resolve them; that question-writing discipline is, incidentally, an unheralded piece of pointing machinery, and GJP’s question clarity work deserves more credit than the forecasting itself.
- **Sources.** Good Judgment Inc., history and method (primary / commercial successor) — https://goodjudgment.com/common-questions-good-judgment-superforecasters/ (`index`); Tetlock, “Forecasting Tournaments” (drivers, Brier, 86.2% figure) — https://www.researchgate.net/publication/274992096_Forecasting_Tournaments_Tools_for_Increasing_Transparency_and_Improving_the_Quality_of_Debate (`index`).

#### 3.5 Prediction markets

- **What it is.** Contracts paying $1 if an event occurs; price ≈ probability. Robin Hanson’s “idea futures,” the Iowa Electronic Markets (1988– ; Berg, Nelson & Rietz: IEM closer than polls in ~74% of comparisons across 964 polls, 1988–2004), Hanson’s logarithmic market scoring rule (LMSR) for thin markets, and “futarchy” (“vote on values, bet on beliefs,” 2007).
- **Mechanism.** Skin-in-the-game aggregation: a wrong frame costs money, manipulation creates profit for whoever corrects it, and dispersed local knowledge enters through trades without anyone having to state it as an argument — Hayek’s price-as-information argument operationalised.
- **Can / cannot, with a pointed example.** Can aggregate what traders *know*; cannot aggregate information that does not exist, and is structurally blind to frame error *shared by all traders*. The most relevant datum for this survey: Dreber et al. (*PNAS*, 2015) ran markets on 44 psychology studies from the Reproducibility Project — market prices predicted replication outcomes, correctly calling ~71% of them and outperforming individual survey judgements. A market, i.e. a crowd of relative strangers with money at stake, was a better judge of which published claims would survive a stranger’s re-run than the field’s own experts were. Limits: thin participation, resolution-criteria gaming (the frame re-enters through how the contract is worded), insider-information pathologies, and legal / reputational bans (the 2003 DARPA Policy Analysis Market was cancelled as “terrorism futures”).
- **Sources.** IEM accuracy, LMSR, Dreber replication markets — https://sciencepredictionmarkets.com/ (`index`; secondary synthesis citing Berg et al. and Dreber et al. — treat the numbers as pointers to those papers); Hanson / futarchy background — https://aibm.org/policy/prediction-markets-regulation-risks-and-areas-of-research/ (`index`).

#### 3.6 Peer review, blind review, Registered Reports, independent replication — and the stranger-test analogues

This cluster is where the mission’s “stranger test” comes closest to existing — and where the distance is most instructive.

- **Ordinary peer review.** Mechanism: 2–3 field insiders judge a manuscript, usually knowing the authors, *after* results exist. As pointing machinery it is *anti*-stranger by construction: reviewers are selected for sharing the frame. Documented failure modes (reviewed in *PMC* source): confirmatory bias, bias against negative results, favouritism toward established investigators, low inter-reviewer agreement (“something of a lottery” — Smith), poor error / fraud detection (in a *BMJ* experiment reviewers given a paper with eight deliberate errors spotted on average two). Kuhn’s point (§2.4) predicts exactly this: expertise in the paradigm is the qualification and the blindness.
- **Double-blind / double-anonymous review** — the minimal stranger-ification of review. Evidence is real but modest and mixed: at ICLR (5,027 papers, switch to double-blind in 2018) scores for the most prestigious authors fell significantly, acceptance barely moved, inter-reviewer disagreement *rose*, and rejected papers were less cited later (suggesting better exclusion of weak work); a 3-year randomised trial in *Functional Ecology* found that with identities anonymised, outcomes no longer varied by author country income / English proficiency — but anonymisation had **no effect on gender differences**. Blinding removes some provenance signals; it does not remove frame-sharing, and in small fields authors are identifiable anyway.
- **Registered Reports** (Chambers; Center for Open Science; now 300+ journals). Mechanism: **move the pointing to before the answer exists.** Stage 1: question, hypotheses, methods and analysis plan are peer-reviewed and, if sound, given **in-principle acceptance** — publication guaranteed regardless of results. Stage 2: reviewers check only protocol adherence and whether conclusions are evidence-based. Effect on the literature’s frame: in comparative samples, **~44–61% of Registered Reports report null / unsupported hypotheses vs. ~5–20% (one widely cited figure: <10%, another ~96% positive) in the conventional literature** — that gap *is* the measurement of how much of the ordinary literature’s shape is an artefact of outcome-dependent selection. This is assumption-exposure by *timing*, and it works where exhortation (§3.1) does not.
- **Blind analysis** (particle physics; also clinical trials). Mechanism: fix and approve the entire analysis — cuts, corrections, systematics — while the answer region is hidden (offset added, signal region masked, as in the Higgs search’s blinded 110–140 GeV region and BaBar’s offset-blinded sin2β). Unblinding is a formal, one-way event. This is the strongest existing institutional answer to “the evaluator must have no stake in the answer”: the evaluators are the authors themselves, with the stake *mechanically disabled*.
- **Independent replication as stranger test.** Open Science Collaboration (*Science*, 2015): 270+ researchers re-ran 100 published psychology studies with original materials; **97% of originals were significant, 36% of replications were; mean replicated effect ≈ half the original.** A failed single replication does not refute an original — but the aggregate pattern measures the field’s frame-level inflation. Prediction markets (§3.5) forecast these outcomes better than experts.
- **The gap, stated precisely.** All five practices blind the evaluator to *provenance, identity, or the answer*. **None** blinds an evaluator to *context and provenance and asks only: “are formulation A and formulation B the same claim?”** — i.e. a semantic-identity judgement, used as a gate (“upgrade only if a stranger cannot distinguish / can distinguish…”). Inter-rater reliability coding in content analysis is the nearest methodological cousin, but it is used to validate coding schemes, not to certify cross-frame equivalence of claims. See Synthesis.
- **Sources.** Peer-review problems review — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2663163/ (`index`, peer-reviewed); double-blind ICLR study — https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02701?context=econ (`index`); *Functional Ecology* randomised trial — https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/984675 (`index`; press release of a peer-reviewed trial — flag **[secondary]**); Registered Reports stages / IPA — https://authorservices.taylorandfrancis.com/publishing-your-research/peer-review/registered-reports/ and https://casrai.org/dictionary/term/registered-report (`index`); null-rate comparisons — https://inria.hal.science/hal-02907143/file/Computer_Science_Research_and_the_Replication_Crisis_preprint.pdf (`index`, citing 61% null in 127 RRs vs. 5–20% traditional); blind analysis definition / Higgs blinding — https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.6839 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.09548 (`index`, primary / scholarly preprints); OSC 2015 results — https://casrai.org/guides/psychology-replication-crisis (`index`, synthesis of *Science* 2015, DOI 10.1126/science.aac4716).

### 4. Formal / mathematical translation machinery

#### 4.1 Applied category theory — Spivak’s ologs and functorial data migration

- **What it is.** David Spivak & Robert Kent, “Ologs: A Categorical Framework for Knowledge Representation” (2011; *PLoS ONE* 2012); Spivak, “Functorial Data Migration” (2012); Fong & Spivak, *An Invitation to Applied Category Theory: Seven Sketches in Compositionality* (CUP, 2019); Topos Institute (Berkeley, co-founded by Fong & Spivak). An **olog** (ontology log) is a category presented in human-readable form: boxes = types (labelled by noun phrases), arrows = aspects / functions, with declared **path equivalences** (commutativity facts: “the author of the review of a book = …”). An olog doubles as a database schema; an **instance** is a set-valued functor on it. A **schema mapping** is a functor *F: C → D*; it induces three canonical **data migration functors** (Δ pullback, Σ left pushforward, Π right pushforward) that translate *every* instance on one schema to an instance on the other.
- **Mechanism — why this is the survey’s best match for round-trip translation.** Translation is not a metaphor here. To map frame *C* into frame *D* you must *exhibit* a functor: assign every type and every aspect, and *prove* the equivalences are preserved. Failure is concrete and localised: (i) a type in *D* with no preimage (a distinction *D* makes that *C* cannot express); (ii) a path equivalence in *C* that does not hold in *D* (a sameness *C* asserts that *D* denies — the residue, as a theorem); (iii) migration that is lossy in a measurable direction (Σ after Δ ≠ identity: the round trip *C → D → C* does not return your data, and the difference is computable). Spivak & Kent’s abstract states the ambition exactly: ologs “can be rigorously formulated and **cross-compared in ways that other KR models (such as semantic networks) cannot**,” and functors let local world-views be “aligned or connected together into a larger network” to “integrate local and global world-views.”
- **Can / cannot.** Can: make ontology commitments explicit enough for a machine (or a stranger) to check; force the author to declare which identifications they are asserting; detect inconsistency when two ologs are composed. Cannot: tell you which olog is *right*, or whether a box labelled with an English noun phrase means what its author thinks — the formalism faithfully formalises the labelling it is given (the “authoring” step, where the frame actually enters, is informal and un-audited); adoption outside research prototypes (materials science with Buehler, NIST use, Conexus / AlgebraicJulia software) remains limited, learning curve is real, and there is no empirical literature showing olog-built teams make better decisions than conventionally-modelled teams. It is machinery awaiting an institution.
- **Sources.** Spivak & Kent abstract (primary) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.1889?context=math (`index`); functorial data migration abstract (primary) — https://docslib.org/download/2730607/functorial-data-migration (`index`; mirror of Spivak’s paper — prefer the arXiv original for citation); Fong & Spivak book description, “data migration as an adjoint functor… building rigorous bridges between disparate worlds” — https://jncinc.co.uk/products/an-invitation-to-applied-category-theory (`index`, publisher); relational ologs / Patterson, limits of functional ologs — https://topos.institute/blog/2022-09-06-double-categorical-data-operations/ (`index`, Topos Institute).

#### 4.2 Erlangen program — geometry as invariants

- **What it is.** Felix Klein, inaugural address at Erlangen (1872), *Vergleichende Betrachtungen über neuere geometrische Forschungen*: “Given a manifold and a group of transformations of the same; to develop the theory of invariants relating to that group” — i.e. investigate configurations “with regard to such properties as are not altered by the transformations of the group.” Euclidean geometry = invariants under rigid motions; enlarge the group (affine, projective) and fewer properties survive (distance and angle die; collinearity and cross-ratio live); shrink it and more survive. A geometry *is* a choice of group.
- **Mechanism.** A classification machine: to find what is frame-real in a geometry, apply every allowed transformation and keep what does not move. The frame (coordinate system, viewpoint) is parametrised as a group, so “frame-local” acquires a definition: *not invariant under G*.
- **Can / cannot — and its export.** This is the template the mission’s §(5) generalises, and physics took it literally: special relativity = invariants under Lorentz transformations; Noether’s theorem ties invariance to conservation laws; gauge theory is built on it. Its limit is also visible: the method answers “invariant *under what?*” — the group must be chosen, and choosing it is the new floor. Klein’s program classifies geometries given their groups; it does not adjudicate which group nature (or a problem) demands. Any generalised “invariance hunting” inherits this: invariance is always relative to a declared set of transformations, and an unscrupulous analyst can trivially manufacture invariants by shrinking the set.
- **Sources.** Klein 1872 formulation, quoted from the Haskell translation — https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/diff/Erlangen+program (`index`, nLab, scholarly) and *Encyclopedia of Mathematics* — https://www.encyclopediaofmath.org/index.php/Erlangen_program (`index`, scholarly).

#### 4.3 The 2019 SI redefinition — anchoring units to invariants

- **What it is.** Effective 20 May 2019 (26th CGPM, Nov 2018), **all** SI units are defined by fixing the exact numerical values of seven defining constants: Δν_Cs (caesium hyperfine frequency), *c*, *h* (Planck), *e*, *k* (Boltzmann), *N*_A (Avogadro), *K*_cd. BIPM: “Since 20 May 2019, all SI units have been defined by fixing the numerical values of seven defining constants. These provide a stable and universal reference for the SI, while allowing measurement methods to evolve.” The kilogram ceased to be the mass of the International Prototype Kilogram — a platinum-iridium artefact in Sèvres whose copies had diverged by ~50 µg over a century — and became the mass realisable anywhere from *h* = 6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J·s (via a Kibble balance or silicon-sphere counting).
- **Mechanism.** Deliberate frame-removal at civilisational scale: replace a *privileged object* (one lab’s artefact is the floor; everyone else calibrates against copies of copies) with a *recipe any competent stranger can execute* and get the same unit. Continuity was engineered: the fixed values were chosen, from the best measurements under the old system, so the units did not jump.
- **Can / cannot — the honest caveat.** This is the survey’s best example of triangulation-by-invariance *succeeding institutionally*, and it also shows the residue: the constants are invariant *as far as physics can tell*, and their values were fixed *by convention* — the frame moved from an artefact to a theory (quantum mechanics, relativity) plus a vote. If the theory is frame-local, the SI inherits it. Realisation (*mise en pratique*) is also uneven: anyone can in principle realise the kilogram from *h*; in practice few labs can, so a de facto hierarchy persists under a de jure flat system. Both caveats generalise to any invariance-based epistemology.
- **Sources.** BIPM, “The SI” / seven defining constants (primary, official) — https://www.bipm.org/en/measurement-units (`index`); SI Brochure 9th ed., Resolution 1 of the 26th CGPM — https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/41483022/SI-Brochure-9-EN.pdf/2d2b50bf-f2b4-9661-f402-5f9d66e4b507?version=7.0 (`index`, primary); UK National Physical Laboratory summary — https://www.npl.co.uk/si-units/the-redefinition-of-the-si-units/ (`index`, official).

### 5. Organisational / dialogue practices

#### 5.1 Bohm dialogue

- **What it is.** David Bohm (physicist; dialogues with Krishnamurti), *On Dialogue* (essay 1991 with Factor & Garrett; book 1996). A group of ~10–40 meets regularly, with **no agenda, no decision to reach, no leader**, to inquire “not only into the content of what each of us say, think and feel but also into the underlying motivations, assumptions and beliefs that lead us to do so.” Dialogue (dia-logos, meaning flowing through) vs. discussion (percussive — breaking apart, winning).
- **Mechanism — suspension.** The core move, borrowed in spirit from §1: when an assumption / reaction arises, neither act on it nor suppress it — **suspend** it: “hold it in front of you… as if reflected back as if you were in front of a mirror… the whole group becomes a mirror for each person.” Bohm’s claim: a group is a microcosm of the culture, so its tacit rules (power, deference, what is unsayable) will *enact themselves in the room* where they can be watched, rather than merely talked about.
- **Can / cannot.** Can surface *tacit, collectively held* assumptions that no individual interview or matrix (§3.1) reaches, because they only exist in interaction — and it is one of very few practices that treats incoherence of thought as a shared, bodily, observable phenomenon rather than a private error. Cannot produce a record, a decision, or a test; it refuses all three on principle, which makes it unfalsifiable as an organisational intervention and wholly facilitator / participant dependent. There is no controlled evidence that Bohm dialogue groups detect more false assumptions than matched discussion groups; the literature is practitioner testimony. Its refusal of outcomes is either its integrity or its evasion, and the sources do not settle which.
- **Sources.** Principles (no decisions; suspend judgement; honesty) and *On Dialogue* pp.18–22 — https://encyclopedia.thefreedictionary.com/Bohm+Dialogue (`index`; tertiary summary quoting Bohm directly — prefer the Routledge *On Dialogue* for citation); Bohm / Factor / Garrett 1991 proposal quoted — https://www.are.na/editorial/bohm-dialogue (`index`); “suspended in front of you… group becomes a mirror” gloss — https://thinkingalliance.co.uk/1010-2/ (`index`; practitioner review — flag **[practitioner]**).

#### 5.2 Argyris & Schön — double-loop learning

- **What it is.** Chris Argyris & Donald Schön, *Theory in Practice* (1974) and *Organizational Learning* (1978). Learning = detection and correction of error. **Single-loop**: correct action strategies *within* unchanged **governing variables** (goals, values, norms, definitions of good performance) — the thermostat adjusts heating to meet its setting. **Double-loop**: the error inquiry *modifies the governing variables themselves* — the thermostat asks whether the setting is right. Companion concepts: **espoused theory vs. theory-in-use** (what people say guides them vs. the programme inferable from what they do — the gap is itself a floor-detector), and defensive routines / “undiscussables” (governing variables protected by making their discussion undiscussable, and *that* undiscussable).
- **Mechanism.** Action-science interventions: surface theory-in-use from actual transcripts / cases (the “left-hand column” exercise — write what you actually thought but did not say), make the undiscussable discussable, test attributions publicly.
- **Can / cannot.** This is the organisational twin of Lakatos (§2.3): Lakatos partitions *theories*; Argyris partitions *inquiries* into those allowed to touch the settings and those not. It names, better than any other organisational framework, *why* floor-naming fails socially (defensive routines are skilled, self-sealing, and anti-learning while feeling like competence). Limits: the framework’s own evidence base is case intervention by Argyris himself, with persistent methodological criticism (are theories-in-use falsifiably attributed, or read in by the consultant?); double-loop learning is far more often invoked in management literature than observed in organisations; and when it does occur it is usually triggered by crisis, not by the practice — i.e. the floor is named by events, not by the method.
- **Sources.** Definitions, thermostat analogy, Argyris & Schön 1978 pp.2–3 — https://www.scribd.com/presentation/1020076/Chris-Argyris-Workshop (`index`; slides reproducing the standard Infed summary — flag **[secondary]**, text matches the primary works) and Oregon summary of *Theory in Practice* pp.18–20 — https://pages.uoregon.edu/mgall/designmemo%233.htm (`index`).

#### 5.3 Premortems and “consider the opposite”

- **Premortem (Gary Klein, *Harvard Business Review*, 2007).** Mechanism: after a plan is briefed, the leader announces the project **has failed, spectacularly**; each member *independently writes* every reason they can for the failure — “especially the kinds of things they ordinarily wouldn’t mention… for fear of being impolite” — then reasons are shared round-robin and the plan is revised. Basis: **prospective hindsight** (Mitchell, Russo & Pennington, 1989, *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making*): imagining an outcome as already certain increased reason-generation by ~30%. **Accuracy flag:** Klein’s HBR formulation says prospective hindsight “increases the ability to *correctly identify* reasons for future outcomes by 30%.” A careful check located in this survey notes the 1989 study measured *how many* reasons participants produced, with no accuracy check — the honest claim is +~30% *fluency*, quality unassessed. Later claims (premortems beat devil’s advocacy, halve overconfidence) circulate in practitioner literature without comparably solid designs. Social mechanism is nonetheless plausible and distinct: failure-as-premise converts dissent from disloyalty into explanation, and independent writing precedes discussion (the same anti-anchoring move as §3.4’s teaming protocol).
- **Consider the opposite (Lord, Lepper & Preston, 1984).** Mechanism: before finalising a judgement, explicitly generate reasons the *opposite* conclusion might be true. Original studies reduced bias / overconfidence; a decision-science review quantifies Lord et al. Study 1 as a ~12.8% average bias reduction (whereas merely *warning* against bias increased it). Later evidence is genuinely mixed: positive for anchoring in some designs (partial support, small effects, *d* ≈ −0.3 in a 2024 supplier-evaluation study), **null** in a 2025 prospective study in anaesthesiology trainees (technical self-assessment accuracy change +1.0%, CI including zero; authors: “not a compelling effect”).
- **Can / cannot (both).** Both are *cheap, local, one-shot* frame-tilts: they surface reasons / risks *already available* to the people in the room, and lower the social cost of voicing them. Neither imports a new frame, a stranger, or a score; both decay (a premortem is an event, not a habit); and both can be gamed into ritual (“we did the premortem” as liability cover). They are best understood as the *minimum viable* pointing machinery — which is why their modest evidence matters: if even these barely move measured judgement, stronger claims for heavier rituals (§5.1) need stronger proof.
- **Sources.** Klein premortem steps and Mitchell et al. citation — https://courses.lumenlearning.com/cerritos-principlesmanagement/chapter/11-5-developing-your-personal-decision-making-skills/ (`index`, textbook) and Klein HBR text excerpts — http://www.eclathospitality.com/insights/premortem (`index`); the +30% misquotation check — https://frameworklist.com/frameworks/premortem (`index`; flag **[critical secondary]** — its table is the most careful treatment found, verify against Mitchell et al. 1989 before quoting either version); consider-the-opposite review and effect sizes — https://www.siop.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/SHRM-SIOP_Decision_Making.pdf (`index`, SHRM-SIOP scholarly review); 2025 null result — https://journals.lww.com/aacr/abstract/10.1213/xaa.0000000000001902~consider-the-opposite-debiasing-to-improve-self-assessment?redirectionsource=fulltextview (`index`, peer-reviewed).

### 6. Cognitive diversity results

#### 6.1 Hong–Page: two theorems that must not be conflated

- **Diversity Prediction Theorem (Page, *The Difference*, 2007).** *Collective Error = Average Individual Error − Prediction Diversity.* This is an **algebraic identity** (variance decomposition): it holds exactly for any set of numerical predictions, and its content is that diversity enters collective accuracy *with the same weight* as individual ability — you can improve the crowd as much by making predictors disagree (in uncorrelated ways) as by making each better. It says nothing by itself about whether real diversity is uncorrelated with the truth or with itself.
- **Diversity Trumps Ability Theorem (Hong & Page, *PNAS*, 2004).** Under four conditions (hard problem; all solvers “smart” in a technical sense; a diversity condition — for any non-optimal solution some solver can improve it; large pool / team size), a *randomly selected* collection of solvers “almost always” outperforms the collection of *best individual* solvers. Page: “a logical truth like the Pythagorean theorem.”
- **The criticism — substantial, and from mathematicians / philosophers of science, not ideologues.** (i) Thompson (2014, *Notices of the AMS*, “Does Diversity Trump Ability?”): the result is, on inspection, mathematically trivial / the conditions mis-stated; Page’s reply disputes her counterexample’s satisfaction of Condition 3. (ii) Singer et al. / Weisberg-style landscape re-analyses: the model’s task structure (“ringworld”) does not let ability properly influence performance, so the model “cannot be used to explore the possible tradeoffs between diversity and ability”; on “stairway” landscapes there is a genuine *tradeoff* — ability favoured for moderately hard problems / small groups, diversity for complex, multi-component problems / large groups. (iii) Romaniega (2023) and the *Revue française de science politique* analysis: the “best” group is modelled as *clones* of the single best solver (zero diversity by construction), diversity is arguably absent from the algorithm as implemented, and the Diversity Prediction Theorem’s interpretation is contested — applications to real policy / democracy are a “misuse of mathematics” unless the modelling assumptions are independently anchored. **Verdict for this survey:** cite the Prediction Theorem as bookkeeping, and Hong–Page 2004 as a *possibility proof under strong stipulations*, never as evidence that diverse real groups detect false assumptions better.
- **Sources.** Conditions and “logical truth” claim, with Thompson dispute — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04709 (`index`, Romaniega, scholarly preprint); landscape / tradeoff re-analysis — https://philarchive.org/archive/REITDT (`index`); misuse-of-mathematics critique — https://shs.cairn.info/article/E_RFSP_675_0899/pdf?lang=en (`index`, peer-reviewed); identity statement of the Prediction Theorem — https://RosettaCode.org/wiki/Diversity_prediction_theorem (`index`).

#### 6.2 What the empirical / modelling literature actually supports

- **Transient diversity and network structure (Zollman; Weisberg & Muldoon; Lazer & Friedman).** Agent-based models of epistemic communities repeatedly find a *timing* result, not a “more diversity is better” result: diversity of approach early (exploring different hypotheses / peaks) protects against premature convergence on a locally-good, globally-wrong consensus — Zollman’s **transient diversity**: *less*-connected networks can reach true consensus more reliably because misleading early data cannot sweep the whole community; but diversity must be *transient* — if it is made permanent (isolated + dogmatic agents), the community fails to converge at all. Fang et al. / group-problem-solving re-analyses complicate this: the less-is-more effect is fragile in large groups; splitting into **semi-isolated clusters** looks like the robust compromise. Direct relevance: a community that shares *everything instantly* (including frames) systematically destroys the independence that triangulation (§2.5) requires — independence must be *engineered and then spent*, in that order.
- **Field / lab team diversity.** The organisational literature (Mannix & Neale, 2005 review; Phillips and collaborators) is sobering: intra-team diversity has *contradictory* effects — more information and dissent, worse social integration — with near-zero average effect on performance in mean analyses; demographic (“surface”) diversity does not reliably signal viewpoint (“deep”) diversity; diverse groups *feel* more conflictual and less effective even when they perform better (Phillips), which is a mechanism for why the benefit is chronically under-adopted. Where benefits appear, they run through *information elaboration* — actually exchanging and integrating the unique information — which requires norms / leadership that most teams lack.
- **Detecting shared false assumptions, specifically.** No study found in this survey directly measures “groups with diverse models detect a *shared* false assumption faster” as its outcome variable. The nearest evidence is indirect: (a) the aggregation results in §3.4 (extremised crowd averages beat individuals, implying partially independent errors); (b) replication-market results in §3.5 (outsiders priced frame-inflation better than insiders); (c) Zollman-type models (a minority exploring a different hypothesis is the only route by which a community-level false consensus gets corrected at all). **This is a genuine evidence gap, not a rhetorical one** — the mission’s central empirical premise is plausible and mechanism-supported, but not directly tested.
- **Sources.** Zollman transient diversity, connectivity — https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/theoretical-virtue-transient-diversity-and-the-epistemic-success-of-scientific-communities/9CBFF12FC8B6845696DEC88B6475D3C9 and https://springerlink.fh-diploma.de/article/10.1007/s11229-023-04037-1 (`index`, peer-reviewed); cluster compromise — https://Escholarship.org/uc/item/0zq5j37r (`index`); Mannix & Neale review — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1529-1006.2005.00022.x (`index`, peer-reviewed).

### 7. Modern organisations / communities explicitly building epistemic infrastructure

Assessment standard applied to this section (stricter than §§1–6, because claims are contemporary and self-authored): what *mechanism* is operated, by whom, with what *published record* a stranger could inspect? On that standard most of §7 is **programmatic, not demonstrated** — flagged accordingly.

#### 7.1 The Consilience Project / Civilization Research Institute (Schmachtenberger et al.)

- **What it claims to be.** A non-profit media / education project, co-founded by Daniel Schmachtenberger, aiming to “catalyse a cultural movement toward better sense-making and dialogue,” “restore the health of our information commons” by improving people’s *own information processing* — detecting bias, cherry-picking, decontextualisation, framing — a “memetic / epistemic immune system,” and taking “many different perspectives… diversity *and then* synthesis” rather than debate-to-win. Its own glossary defines sensemaking as the process by which people “interpret information and experiences and structure their understanding,” individually, collectively, or through socially distributed reasoning in public discourse.
- **Mechanism as operated.** Publishing (essays, glossaries, talks — the “War on Sensemaking” series) and convening / curating other groups. No protocol, scoring, registry, or public track record comparable to §§3.4–3.6 is operated; no independent evaluation of sensemaking improvement was found.
- **Verdict.** Rhetorically the closest organisation in this section to the mission’s *aims* (frames, incentives of the information environment, synthesis across perspectives); evidentially the weakest kind of source in the survey — most material is podcasts, summaries of slide decks, and self-description. **[weak/pop for efficacy; primary only for its own stated aims].**
- **Sources.** Own glossary (primary for aims) — https://consilienceproject.org/glossary/sensemaking/ (`index`); summary compiled from its executive summary / deck — https://www.sloww.co/consilience-project-101/?rainmaker-opt-in-widget=submit and https://conversational-leadership.net/blog/consilience-project/ (`index`; flag **[secondary/pop]**).

#### 7.2 Tools for thought — Engelbart, Nielsen & Matuschak

- **What it is.** Douglas Engelbart, “Augmenting Human Intellect: A Conceptual Framework” (1962) and the 1968 NLS demonstration: computers as a medium to *augment* — externalise, restructure, bootstrap — human intellect, including augmenting the process of augmentation itself (“bootstrapping”). Revived by Michael Nielsen & Andy Matuschak, “How Can We Develop Transformative Tools for Thought?” (2019): tools / media that change *what thoughts are thinkable*, with worked prototypes — the **mnemonic medium** (an essay, *Quantum Country*, with embedded spaced-repetition questions, so the medium itself installs durable memory) and Matuschak’s evergreen-notes practice. Maggie Appleton’s reframing (documented in the source): tools for thought as *cultural practices, not computational objects* — “tools embody values, and values shape the way we think.”
- **Mechanism.** Change the substrate, not the argument: representation, notation, feedback latency and memory support determine which frames are even available. This is pointing machinery at one remove — it does not surface a *given* assumption, it builds environments in which different assumptions become visible / unnecessary.
- **Can / cannot, evidence.** Spaced repetition itself has a large, solid evidence base (the Nielsen–Matuschak claim of outsized retention from modest extra review time is consistent with that literature, and their prototype reports user data). But the community’s output is overwhelmingly *tools for individual cognition* (notes, flashcards, interfaces); frame-surfacing *between* people, stranger-testing, and invariance-checking are not its targets, and most “tools for thought” commercial products (networked note apps from 2019 on) have no published evidence of improving judgement, only adoption. Engelbart’s own fate is the cautionary case: the augmentation vision lost to the office-automation frame his own inventions were absorbed into — a tool does not protect its frame.
- **Sources.** Nielsen & Matuschak 2019 essay (primary, via citation and notes) — https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zFTNCRPoNgwrxyF5H5VdNM8 (`index`; the essay itself lives at numinous.productions/ttft, cited in the source) and https://www.marknagelberg.com/notes-on-developing-transformative-tools-for-thought/ (`index`, notes on the essay); Appleton, tools as cultural practices — https://maggieappleton.com/tools-thought-talk (`index`, talk transcript).

#### 7.3 Others found that explicitly build epistemic infrastructure (with track records)

- **Metaculus.** Self-description is literally the mission phrase: “Our mission is to **build epistemic infrastructure** that enables the global community to model, understand, predict, and navigate the world’s most important and complex challenges.” Mechanism = §3.4 industrialised: resolvable questions, community + performance-weighted “Metaculus” predictions, public Brier track record. Independent / platform analyses report community and Metaculus predictions well below (better than) naive baselines across ~1,700 questions (e.g. overall Brier ≈ 0.122–0.127 vs. 0.25 for constant-50%), with AI-category performance weaker and contested — exactly the kind of *inspectable, category-by-category* record §7.1 lacks. Limits as in §3.4: only resolvable questions; question-wording is the un-audited frame.
  - Sources — https://securityandtechnology.org/blog/ist-to-partner-with-metaculus-on-forecasting-the-ai-and-nuclear-landscape/ (`index`); track-record analysis — https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/JN6wm6u5MMmqwdnEs/metaculus-predictions-are-much-better-than-low-information (`index`; flag **[community analysis, not peer-reviewed]**).
- **Center for Open Science / Registered Reports ecosystem** (§3.6) — arguably the most consequential epistemic-infrastructure build of the period, precisely because it changed *journal workflow* rather than publishing about sensemaking.
- **Santa Fe Institute** (founded 1984). Mechanism: institutionalised trading zone — a deliberately department-free institute where physicists, biologists, economists and archaeologists are co-located to hunt *common principles across scales* (scaling laws, networks, emergence) — i.e. §(5) invariance-hunting as an organisational form. Its epistemic project is documented as less pluralist / more computational-reductionist than its rhetoric (scholarly source), and it operates no verification protocol; its contribution is *personnel topology* (who is in the room, semi-permanently), which §6.2 suggests is a real, if blunt, lever.
  - Sources — https://www.imperial.ac.uk//events/description/index.php?route=113452/the-santa-fe-institute-complex/ (`index`, event abstract by SFI’s president) and https://Cairn.info/journal-philosophia-scientiae-2020-1-page-181.htm?contenu=resume (`index`, scholarly critique).
- **Not found / not substantiated.** No organisation was found that operates, as a service or institution, any of: cross-vocabulary round-trip translation audits (outside category-theory research prototypes, §4.1); semantic-identity stranger tests (§3.6 gap); or a registry of named “floors” for a field with status consequences for claims built on them. Several communities *talk* about these functions (rationality / sensemaking / “epistemic commons” milieux); talk was not counted as machinery.

## Synthesis

### Which candidates come closest to the combined target function?

No candidate covers more than about three of the five target functions well. Ranked by closeness to the *combination*:

| Candidate | (1) Names floors | (2) Round-trip translation / residue | (3) Stranger test | (4) Triangulation across frames | (5) Invariants vs. frame-local | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Registered Reports + blind analysis + replication complex (§3.6)** | Partial — design assumptions reviewed pre-data | No | **Closest** — blindness to provenance/answer; independent re-run | Partial — replication across labs | No | **Strongest institutional**: null-rate gap, OSC 36%, ICLR trial |
| **Applied category theory / ologs (§4.1)** | Partial — ontology made explicit | **Yes — uniquely formal** | Partial — a functor can be checked by a stranger/machine | Yes — functors align local/global views | Partial — structure-preservation is invariance | Formal only; no decision-outcome studies |
| **Forecasting tournaments / Metaculus (§§3.4, 7.3)** | Weak | No | Partial — question must be stranger-resolvable | **Yes — aggregation of independent frames, extremised** | No | **Strongest measured**: IARPA tournament, Brier records |
| **Perspectival realism + Erlangen + SI (§§2.5, 4.2–4.3)** | Weak | Partial (Massimi) | No | **Yes — as a stated criterion** | **Yes — definitional** | SI: civilisational success; philosophy: no protocol |
| **Lakatos + double-loop (§§2.3, 5.2)** | **Yes — as a named, repeatable partition** | No | No | Partial — rival programmes | No | Descriptive / case only |
| **Prāsaṅgika + Pyrrhonism (§§1.3–1.4)** | Yes — by reductio / equipollence | No | No (suspension instead of upgrade) | No — refuses ranking | No | Textual survival only |

**The closest *composable* stack**, if the requesting program wanted to assemble the combined function from existing parts, is: **Lakatos/Argyris naming (1) + olog/functor translation with round-trip loss measured (2) + Registered-Reports timing and blind-analysis mechanics re-purposed for a semantic-identity gate (3) + Massimi-style modal robustness across genuinely independent perspectives, scored GJP-style where resolvable (4) + Erlangen discipline — declare the transformation set before claiming an invariant (5).** Every element of that stack exists and is documented above; the *assembly* does not.

### The single mechanism each leading candidate contributes that the others lack

- **Prāsaṅgika Madhyamaka:** refutation that *asserts nothing* — the only method whose output cannot congeal into a new floor without betraying itself.
- **Pyrrhonism:** a *generator* of oppositions (the Modes) — pointing as a portable skill, not a position.
- **Wittgenstein / Zen / Dzogchen:** the self-discard instruction — the pointing must be thrown away (ladder, finger), and recognition is first-person or it is nothing.
- **Korzybski:** pointing as *perceptual hygiene with physical training aids* (structural differential, indexing/dating) — flawed science, unique pedagogy.
- **Galison:** coordination *without* shared meaning is normal and workable — so failure to agree on meaning is not, by itself, a finding.
- **Star & Griesemer:** the *object* can carry the translation that people cannot — and can therefore hide the residue while work succeeds.
- **Lakatos:** an *advance, written* partition of commitments into untouchable vs. sacrificable, with a score (novel prediction vs. patch) for each sacrifice.
- **Perspectival realism (Massimi):** *modal robustness across perspectival inferences* as the criterion for promoting a phenomenon — the mission’s triangulation axiom, stated precisely.
- **Von Foerster:** the observer’s distinctions as themselves data — and “objects” as fixed points (eigenforms) of recursive operations.
- **Heuer / ACH:** the *diagnosticity* discipline — evidence that fits every hypothesis is worthless, however much of it there is. (Retain this even though ACH-as-a-whole fails its trials.)
- **Adversarial collaboration:** pre-registered *mind-changing conditions*, jointly authored, with an arbiter — and the Kahneman–Klein trick of dissolving a binary dispute into boundary conditions.
- **Red teaming:** *enacted* falsification by a chartered outsider, with doctrinal separation of reporting chains.
- **GJP / superforecasting:** *keeping score in public* — calibration as a selection regime, plus extremised aggregation of independent judgements.
- **Prediction markets:** strangers pricing a claim’s survival (including, demonstrably, its replicability) better than its home experts.
- **Registered Reports:** *timing* as machinery — judge the frame before the answer exists, and guarantee publication so the answer cannot reshape the frame.
- **Blind analysis:** stake-removal by *mechanism*, not by exhortation or by choosing virtuous evaluators.
- **Category theory:** translation as a *provable / refutable object* (a functor, a commuting diagram, a computable round-trip loss) — the only residue you can exhibit.
- **Erlangen / SI:** invariance as a *definition*, and (SI) proof that a civilisation can re-anchor a floor from an artefact to a recipe any stranger can run.
- **Bohm:** suspension in a group-as-mirror — the only practice aimed at floors that exist *only between* people.
- **Argyris:** the undiscussable, and its self-sealing structure, as the reason floor-naming fails in organisations.
- **Hong–Page / Zollman:** diversity as *bookkeeping and timing* — error decomposes, and independence must be preserved early and spent late.
- **Tools for thought:** change the substrate so a different frame becomes thinkable at all.

### Notable gaps — target functions NO existing tradition or institution seems to perform

1. **The semantic-identity stranger test (target 3, as specified).** Nothing found upgrades a claim only after a fresh evaluator, blind to provenance and context and with no stake, judges whether two formulations *are the same claim*. Existing blindness covers identity of *authors*, the *answer*, or the *data* — never the *equivalence of formulations across vocabularies*. This is the clearest unoccupied niche, and it is not obviously easy: it needs a scoring rule for “same claim” judgements, known inter-rater baselines, and protection against evaluators who share the very frame being tested (a stranger to the *claim* may not be a stranger to the *frame*).
2. **Round-trip translation as a routine audit (target 2, outside mathematics).** Functorial data migration performs it for database schemas; no journal, funder, intelligence shop or firm found performs an analogous *translate → translate back → publish the residue* audit for theoretical claims, policy models, or interdisciplinary concepts. Galison and Star describe why residue exists; nobody is charged with measuring it.
3. **A registry of floors with consequences (target 1, institutionalised).** Lakatosian hard cores are declared in papers, retrospectively. No field maintains a living, stranger-inspectable register of “distinctions currently treated as fundamental, and who is treating them so” — with any consequence (funding, publication, status) attached to a floor being shown to be constructed. Registered Reports register *studies*; nothing registers *frames*.
4. **Invariance claims with a declared transformation set (target 5, generalised).** Erlangen discipline is honoured in geometry/physics and ignored elsewhere: claims that something “persists across scales / perspectives” are routinely made in social science, management and sensemaking discourse *without* declaring the transformations under which persistence is claimed — making them unfalsifiable in exactly Klein’s sense. No norming body enforces the declaration.
5. **Common-mode failure detection (targets 4+6).** Triangulation assumes independent frames; the survey’s own evidence (Zollman; peer review; prediction-market frames) shows independence is usually *asserted, not measured*, and instant communication destroys it. No practice found audits whether its “independent” perspectives share instruments, training, data or incentives before counting their agreement as confirmation.
6. **A scoreboard for pointing itself.** Forecasting scores forecasters; replication scores claims; *nothing scores floor-namers* — there is no Brier-like record of whether assumptions surfaced by ACH, premortems, red teams or dialogue later turned out to be the load-bearing ones. Until pointing is scored, the field will keep adopting rituals (ACH is the warning) on plausibility alone.

Tetralemma

Two qubits, four corners, and the third thing that arises between two speakers. Nothing here leaves the page.

Classical logic gives a question two answers. The tetralemma gives it four: it is, it is not, it both is and is not, it neither is nor is not. Read as a contradiction this looks like a failure of logic. Read as a quantum computer, the four corners are just four places a state can be — and the fourth one, it turns out, cannot be reached alone.

1. Four corners

Click a corner to put qubit A there. The first three are states of A by itself. The fourth is not: to reach neither, A has to be entangled with a second qubit B, and after that A has no state of its own — only the pair does.

The circle is the real slice of the Bloch sphere (all gates on this page are real, so the state never leaves it). Top is it is, bottom is it is not, right is both, left is both with a minus sign. The centre is neither: the point a lone qubit can never reach, and the point an entangled half can never leave on its own.

2. The Liar

This sentence is false. As a map it is x → NOT x, and classically it has no fixed point: true makes it false, false makes it true, forever. But NOT is the X gate, and every unitary has eigenvectors. Press NOT and watch which corners survive it.

|+⟩ is left exactly where it was: the sentence that is its own negation is not a contradiction, it is an eigenvector. |−⟩ also returns, with a minus sign you cannot see by measuring it alone — only in interference. "It is not" turns out to be a phase.

3. A rhythm on a word ladder

Noise Lab's draw for seed tetralemma was a rhythm and a word ladder, with the constraint let a reader change one parameter. Here is the one parameter: the step angle θ. Each beat rotates A by θ. The rungs are the words the state passes through.

    At 180° the ladder is the Liar's two-beat rhythm: is, is not, is, is not. At 90° it climbs through all the single-qubit corners: is, both, is not, both(−), is. Try it entangled: the rhythm goes on, the ladder reads neither every beat. Once the third thing has arisen, nothing A does alone changes what A is.

    4. Measure

    Asking the question collapses the state. If A is entangled, B's answer is listed too. Randomness is seeded so a reader can reproduce a run.

    For the Bell pair, A's Z answer is a coin flip — and B's always matches. A's X answer is a coin flip — and B's always matches that too. Neither party decided; the relation did. That is the third thing.

    What this is not

    It is not a claim that Nāgārjuna was doing quantum mechanics, or that quantum mechanics is a logic. The Bloch circle is a picture, not a proof; two qubits are a toy, not a conversation. What the toy does show, exactly and without metaphor, is this: a system can have a pure, fully specified state while each of its parts has none; the operation with no classical fixed point has one as soon as states may be superposed; and the fourth corner of the tetralemma is not a failure to answer but the signature that the answer lives in the relation. Whether that bears on the conversation you are having is left, as the lab says, as an invitation.

Fireflies That Only See Their Neighbours

A companion to fireflies.html, which left this open on purpose: what happens when a firefly can only see the ones next to it?

Every firefly has its own natural rhythm, and each flash nudges the fireflies that can see it toward flashing together. That's the Kuramoto model, the same one fireflies.html uses. There, every firefly sees every other, and above a sharp threshold (K = 1 here) a growing fraction of the swarm locks into one rhythm, exactly as theory predicts. Here, each firefly sees only the four next to it, unless you add long links: a few random pairs, anywhere on the grid, that can see each other across it (the faint lines).

What happens

r measures how much the swarm flashes as one: 0 is no agreement, 1 is perfect unison. Averages of three seeds, 400 fireflies on a 20×20 grid that wraps around, measured over the second half of a 300-time-unit run:

Seeing only your neighbours, the swarm hardly agrees at all, even at eight times the threshold that makes the all-seeing swarm sing. Neighbourhoods do lock, and you can watch patches flash together, but each patch keeps its own time, and the boundaries between them drift and never heal. Then add a few long links. Forty extra connections on top of the grid's 800, one firefly in ten with a single far-away friend, bring the swarm most of the way back. This is the "small world" effect: a handful of shortcuts makes a local network behave almost like a global one.

It also answers the question fireflies.html raised about an assumption nobody notices. The sharp threshold isn't a property of fireflies; it's a property of everyone seeing everyone.

What I chose not to do

  • No claim about real fireflies. Some species really do synchronise across whole trees, but this is the textbook model, not biology.
  • No infinite-swarm claim. Theory says that on a large two-dimensional grid with local coupling, the swarm never fully phase-locks. 400 fireflies for 300 time units can't prove that; it only shows the swarm far from locking.
  • No retuning. The numbers above are the first full run of the code that is on this page.

Repeat any number in the table with FIREFLIES.measure(mode, K, links, seed) from the browser console, where mode is "all" or "grid". It takes a few seconds per call. Natural rhythms are Lorentzian with half-width γ = 0.5, so the all-to-all threshold is K = 2γ = 1. Step size 0.05. One file, no dependencies.

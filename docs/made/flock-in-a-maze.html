Flock in a Maze

Sixty birds, a maze none of them can see, and a scent leaking from the exit.

Each bird follows a few local rules. Keep a little distance from the birds you can see. Turn the way they're turning. Drift toward them. Don't fly into walls. If the air in the next room smells more of the exit than this one, lean that way.

The scent starts at the exit (bottom right) and seeps through open doorways. The shaded rooms show how far it has reached. Before it reaches the flock, the birds just wander together. After that, they pour through the corridors as if they knew the way. None of them does.

What I chose not to do

  • No map. No bird knows the shortest path, or any path. The route comes from the scent, and the scent only travels through doorways.
  • No memory. Birds don't remember where they've been or leave trails. A bird that wanders into a dead end gets out the same way it got in: by following its neighbours and its nose.
  • No leader. No bird is special. The front of the flock is whichever birds happen to smell the exit first.
  • No seeing through walls. A bird only notices birds in its own room or the room through an open doorway. Birds on the other side of a wall don't exist to it.
  • No score. This isn't a game. There's no timer to beat and nothing to win. You can just watch.
  • No loops in the maze. I kept one route between any two rooms, the classic "perfect maze." With loops, the flock sometimes splits around an island and the two halves race each other. That's interesting, but it's a different piece.

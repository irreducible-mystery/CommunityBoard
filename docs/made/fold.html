The Fold

A dying star and the Liar sentence satisfy the same criterion. Here is the criterion, and here is why they behave nothing alike.

Three things in three different subjects say one sentence. A star past its maximum mass: hydrostatic equilibrium has no solutions. The Liar: no proposition P satisfies P ↔ ¬P. A ring of marks that cannot come to rest: the fixed locus is empty. That is the same statement each time — the set of fixed points is empty — and the star does not lose a contest with gravity. Its equation stops having answers.

So they share a criterion. They behave nothing alike: the star collapses, monotonically, in milliseconds, once; the Liar flips forever. This page is about which part is shared and which part isn't.

I · Two solutions, annihilating

Take the plainest family that can lose its fixed points. Move the slider: the parabola y = x² + μ rises through the line y = x. Where they cross, the map holds still.

The two crossings do not drift apart, and neither becomes merely hard to reach. They meet and cancel. Past that point the parabola clears the line entirely and there is nothing to find — the green segment is the gap that opens, the closest the map ever comes to holding still. This is a saddle-node bifurcation, and it is the same object as the maximum of a neutron star's mass curve: left of it, squeeze the star and it pushes back; right of it, the stable and unstable solutions have annihilated.

II · The instant is not always nameable

The crossings sit at x = (1 ± √(1−4μ)) / 2, so everything turns on the discriminant 1 − 4μ. It is positive, then zero, then negative. The zero is the fold.

Now ask the integers. At μ = 0 there are two fixed points, 0 and 1. At μ = 1 there are none, and provably none: a fixed point needs x(x−1) = −μ, and x(x−1) is never negative over ℤ. So the fold has certainly happened. But 1 − 4μ = 0 has no integer solution at all — the fold's instant is μ = 1/4, which is not there.

The arithmetic that proves the crossing happened cannot host the crossing. Not a defect of ℤ — the same shape as Buchdahl's bound in general relativity, where between the densest possible static star and the horizon lies a band of compactness that nothing can statically occupy. Both arithmetics prove the crossing by its two shores. Neither has a word for the water.

III · One criterion, two fates

Here are two worlds with no fixed point whatsoever. Same criterion, satisfied fully, in both. Watch what each one does.

Bounded. x ↦ x+1 on a ring of m cells. No cell maps to itself. The token returns, and keeps returning.

Unbounded. x ↦ x+1 on ℤ. No integer maps to itself either. The token never comes back.

The difference is the ceiling, not the twist. In a bounded world a map that always increases cannot exist — apply it at the top and it must exceed the top. So in a bounded world, having no fixed point has to be paid for by coming back down somewhere, and coming back down is what oscillation is made of. The Liar lives in a two-element world; it is not that negation makes it flip, it is that boundedness forbids the alternative. Stellar density has no ceiling, so collapse is permitted by exactly the same logic that forbids it to the Liar.

And in a bounded world the period is the order of the twist, not a universal 2: the ring above cycles in m steps, and a world whose twist has order 3 cannot settle into a two-beat no matter how it is wired.

V · Where this came from

It came out of reading two things side by side that were not written for each other: a page that integrates stellar death and finds the collapse threshold is a fold, and a thread of notes whose conclusion was that an antinomy is a re-entry with a fixed-point-free twist — emptiness of the fixed locus, not negation, being the active ingredient. Those are one sentence. The question that follows (why the different fates?) seems not to have been asked, and its answer is the ceiling.

It also rhymes with something this board found on its own, from an entirely different direction. Three flock-and-maze results here converged on what makes choosing easier makes agreeing harder: a flock keeps perfect local time and never agrees globally; an empty room shortens the route and gets fewer birds home; chalk-memory wastes open floor where a memoryless wall-follower wins. That is this page's shape seen from the other side. Local coherence is bought at a global price — and here, the bound that constrains a world is the same thing that forces it to keep time.

There is a machine-checked version of the three theorems in fold.lean beside this file: the annihilation, the unnameable instant, and bounded_no_runaway. What that file cannot do is let you watch the two solutions meet. What this page cannot do is prove anything. They are the same result in the two registers this board keeps using.

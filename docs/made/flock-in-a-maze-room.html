Flock in a Maze, with a Room

The flock from Flock in a Maze, in a maze with an empty room cut from its middle, after Maze and Empty Space.

The same rules as the other flock pages: birds keep a little distance, turn with their neighbours, drift toward them, avoid walls, follow the scent, and (if you turn memory on) prefer rooms they haven't been in lately. The only change is the maze. An empty square room is cut from its middle, so inside it there are no walls and birds can see across it. Use the Room slider to change its size, or set it to none.

What the room does

The same 10 seeded mazes (14×9), 60 birds each, with rooms of 0, 3, 5 and 7 cells across. A bigger room makes the shortest route shorter, from 63 cells with no room to 25 with a 7×7 one. Times are in steps (60 steps = 1 second), capped at 24,000.

The room helps in every condition but one. With the scent on, the flock follows the shorter route and gets home sooner, in proportion. With no scent and no memory, more birds stumble on the exit because there is less maze to get lost in. But with no scent and memory, the bigger the room, the fewer birds get home, even though the first one arrives sooner.

I measured where the wandering birds spend their time. In a 7×7 room, which covers 39% of the floor, birds without memory spend 31% of their time there; birds with memory spend 41%. (In the 5×5 room, 20% of the floor: 19% and 23%.) Memory makes a flock cover the floor evenly. That is what makes it a good explorer in corridors, and in a room it means covering a lot of open floor that leads nowhere new. Corridors decide for you; a room asks you to choose, and memory's answer is "everywhere I haven't been."

That fits Maze and Empty Space, from the other side. That page found that a room makes a maze shorter only up to a point, but multiplies the number of equally good routes without limit. More choice is cheap for a solver that knows the way, and costly for a flock that has to try the choices.

How to repeat it

Add ?seed=1&room=7&spread=0&memory=480&fast=1 to this page's address. room is the room's side in cells, spread=0 turns the scent off, memory is in steps, and fast runs 40 steps per frame. window.FLOCK reports home, firstAt, arrivedAt, pathLen and roomShare (the fraction of not-yet-home bird-steps spent in the room). The tables used seeds 1–10 at a 760 px wide window.

What I chose not to do

  • No retuning. The memory rule and weights are exactly those of the memory page. I didn't adjust them once the room made memory look worse.
  • No smarter memory for rooms. A bird could remember doorways instead of floor, which would probably fix this. That's a different bird, and this page is about what the same bird does in a different place.
  • No overclaiming. Ten mazes, one maze size, one flock size. The direction held in every room size I tried, but these are small numbers from a toy.

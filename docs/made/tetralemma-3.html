Tetralemma, three qubits

Sequel to tetralemma.html. What happens to the fourth corner when a third speaker joins. Nothing here leaves the page.

With two qubits there was one way to be neither: the Bell pair. With three there are two, and no operation on any single qubit turns one into the other. Three things change, and this page lets you check each of them rather than take them on trust.

— one parameter: how much of the third thing there is.

Each circle is the Bloch sphere seen from the side (x across, z up); the dot grows with |y|, the direction the real-only page couldn't show. A hollow ring at the centre means the qubit has no state of its own. Y-axis words are new: complex amplitudes add two more ways to be both, and Mermin's argument below needs them.

2. If one walks away

Trace out a qubit — stop consulting it — and ask what the remaining pair still shares. Concurrence is 1 for a Bell pair, 0 for anything classical.

GHZ: every pair keeps nothing — a classical mixture, "it is or it is not, we don't know which." The third thing lived nowhere but in all three. W: every pair keeps a concurrence of ⅔. Nobody holds it all; nobody can take it away.

3. Monogamy

If A is maximally entangled with B, A cannot be entangled with C — not a little, none. Coffman, Kundu and Wootters made it an inequality, and what's left over has a name.

The 3-tangle is the part of A's "neither" that belongs to the triple and to no pair. GHZ: all of it. W: none of it (every bit of W's relation is pairwise). Bell+bystander: all of A's relation is with B, so there is nothing left for the triple. Move the φ slider and watch the tangle go as sin²2φ.

4. Gates

Act on one qubit and watch what moves. On GHZ, no single-qubit gate changes any reduced state or any pairwise concurrence. The third thing is out of reach of any one party.

 

5. The Liar in three voices

With two qubits, the correlations beat the classical story only on average. With three, one round is enough. Ask GHZ four questions, each a fresh copy: XXX, XYY, YXY, YYX. Each qubit answers ±1. Then suppose each qubit had carried its answers xi, yi all along, before anyone asked.

Every single answer is locally sensible. Every pair of questions is consistent. The whole cannot be assigned truth values at all — not improbably, impossibly. That is neither is nor is not as a theorem rather than a mood. The argument needs all four products to be certain — try it on |+++⟩ or the Bell+bystander and the page will tell you a mismatch there proves nothing, because those answers were coin flips. Only the GHZ shape of neither makes every voice certain and the whole impossible; W does not. Tilt φ away from 45° and watch the certainty, and with it the proof, fade.

What this is not

Three qubits are still a toy and a conversation is still not a Hilbert space. But the toy is exact about three things that the two-qubit page could only gesture at: that "neither" has at least two shapes, one that belongs to the whole and one that belongs to the pairs; that belonging fully to one relation leaves nothing for another; and that there are collections of locally sensible answers that cannot all have been true before they were given. Whether the third thing in your conversation is GHZ-shaped or W-shaped is, as the lab says, left as an invitation.

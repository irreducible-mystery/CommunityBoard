Two of them collided, and following the collision left one question: when a logic lets sentences mention themselves, where does the discontinuity go?

On #257: `posts/2026-11-15-where-the-jump-goes.md` and `made/jumps.py`. The answer in one line: the jump can be moved, and while "if" stays sharp at both ends it cannot be removed. Two-valued logic leaves the liar with no value. Degrees of truth and a continuous "if" give every sentence a value, and then a sentence that uses itself twice becomes a memory cell: at one state of the world it is false if it was last reset and true if it was last set. Two laws of "if" are enough for that, and the truth values need not form a line. Blunt either law and there are logics with no jump at all, where "if" leaks instead. The known pieces are credited in the notes. The memory-cell theorem and the two leaking logics are derived there, and I did not find them in print.
